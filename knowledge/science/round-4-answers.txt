QUESTION B3 - BREED ALLELE FREQUENCIES: TBX3, MC1R, SLC45A2, TIGER EYE (2026-10-15)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Her answer, as she sent it (tables reflowed for plain text). She said first: she could
verify the primary papers for sunshine and take the frequencies Avila et al. give in the
main text, but could not retrieve Supplementary Tables S3/S4 or the Mack et al. tiger-eye
paper, and would not invent the missing values.

Data source and definitions
  Unless stated otherwise, all frequencies are allele frequencies, not shares of
  carriers, from Avila et al. 2022 (Genes 13:1641; PMC9498372), calculated after removing
  first-degree relatives within breed; the filtered dataset had 6,878 horses across 28
  breeds. Sample sizes and exact per-breed values are in Supplementary Tables S1-S4.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9498372/ ; https://doi.org/10.3390/genes13091641

TBX3 / dun: D, nd1, nd2
  D dun dilution plus primitive markings; nd1 non-dun with primitive markings; nd2 non-dun
  without. Alternative alleles; per breed they sum to 1.0, subject to rounding.
  Values stated in the main text:
    Pura Raza Espanola   D not stated   nd1 0.79   nd2 0.20   (the remaining 0.01 is
                         presumably D, not stated; confirm in Supplementary Table S4)
    Norwegian Fjord      D 1.0          nd1 0      nd2 0      (D fixed)
    Gypsy Vanner         D 0            nd1 0      nd2 1.0    (nd2 fixed)
    Tennessee Walking    D 0.0060 (lowest D reported); nd1 and nd2 not stated
    Gypsy Cob            nd1 0.017 (lowest nd1 reported); D and nd2 not stated
  D was not detected in Arabian, Dutch Warmblood, Gypsy Vanner, Hanoverian, Knabstrupper,
  Oldenburg, Percheron, Standardbred or Thoroughbred. nd1 was absent only from Gypsy
  Vanner and Norwegian Fjord; nd2 only from Norwegian Fjord.
  Modelling advice: three alleles; D dominant over both; nd1 dominant over nd2 for
  primitive markings.

MC1R / extension: E, e, ea
  Avila et al. tested E, e (p.S83F) and ea (p.D84N). Both e and ea are loss-of-function
  chestnut alleles; e/e, e/ea and ea/ea are chestnut.
    Knabstrupper   ea 0.035 (filtered)
    Paint Horse    ea not separately reported; one e/ea among 2,076 unfiltered, 1/4,152
                   = 0.00024 unfiltered
    Percheron      one E/ea among 52 unfiltered, 1/104 = 0.0096 unfiltered
    Quarter Horse  one E/ea and one e/ea among 5,512 unfiltered, 2/11,024 = 0.00018
                   unfiltered
  The filtered ea figures for these three are in Supplementary Table S4, not retrieved.
  e ranged from 0.76 in Pony of the Americas to 0.12 in Percheron; the full breed-by-breed
  e values are in Supplementary Table S4. A complete E/e/ea table for every breed cannot
  be given without estimating.

SLC45A2: cream Cr and pearl prl
  Alleles of one locus, alternatives to wild type.
  prl, filtered allele frequencies: Andalusian 0.12, Gypsy Cob 0.017, Gypsy Vanner
  0.015, Lusitano 0.064, Paint Horse 0.0070, Pura Raza Espanola 0.11, Quarter Horse
  0.00031.
  Cr occurred in 25 of 28 breeds, absent from Hanoverian, Knabstrupper and Percheron;
  range 0.42 in Lusitano to 0.013 in Standardbred. The breed-by-breed Cr values, including
  Paint Horse and Quarter Horse, are in Supplementary Table S4, not retrieved.
  Allele set at minimum: N, Cr (incomplete dominant), prl (recessive in single copy), Csun
  (proposed). Cr/prl can be dilute.

Sunshine: which frequency claim is right?
  Both statements describe different things; the population-sequencing result is the one
  that addresses allele frequency.
  - Holl et al. 2019 identified SLC45A2 c.568G>A (p.Gly190Arg), proposed as Csun, in a
    small phenotyped dataset of about 130 horses. No horse was homozygous; it was proposed
    to cause a pearl-like recessive dilution in compound heterozygotes. It did not
    establish that sunshine is rare across breeds.
    https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31006892/ ; https://doi.org/10.1111/age.12790
  - Durward-Akhurst et al. 2024 sequenced 605 horses from 48 breeds: 146 heterozygotes and
    390 homozygotes for the variant. The non-reference A allele was the major allele in
    all 12 breed groups examined, above 0.86 in Arabian, Belgian, Morgan, Thoroughbred and
    Quarter Horse. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11006912/ ;
    https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-57872-8
  Correct interpretation: c.568G>A is not rare population-wide. Its status as the causal
  sunshine dilution is uncertain; Durward-Akhurst et al. say more work is needed. Either
  exclude Csun until validated, or include it as putative, with no dilution by default.
  "Very rare, in one lineage" is not supported as a population-frequency claim; it may
  reflect the discovery context.

Tiger eye: TE1 and TE2 in Puerto Rican Paso Fino
  Primary paper: Mack et al. 2017, "Two variants in SLC24A5 are associated with
  'Tiger-Eye' iris pigmentation in Puerto Rican Paso Fino Horses", G3 7:2799-2806. Full
  text not retrieved; no verified whole-breed frequency, and allele versus carrier
  frequency cannot be told. Wolfsberger et al. 2022 studied selection around SLC24A5 in
  Puerto Rican horses but could not evaluate Mack et al.'s four markers, not being in its
  data. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8752667/
  Established: TE1 and TE2 are associated with tiger-eye iris pigmentation in PRPF; most
  tiger-eyed horses in Mack et al. were homozygous for one allele or compound
  heterozygous. Modelling advice: two alleles of one SLC24A5 eye locus; no effect on body,
  mane, tail, hoof, feathering or skin colour.

What these loci do not affect
  None of TBX3, MC1R, SLC45A2 or SLC24A5 has an established effect on height, speed,
  muscle, bone, hoof shape or feathering. Blue eyes may occur with strong SLC45A2
  dilution; tiger eye affects the iris; MC1R and TBX3 have no established eye effect.

Missing data to obtain
  1. Avila et al. 2022 Supplementary Table S4: filtered D, nd1, nd2, E, e, ea, Cr and prl
     for all 28 breeds.
  2. Mack et al. 2017: PRPF sample size, TE1 and TE2 genotype counts, and whether values
     are allele frequencies or carrier proportions.

What the data does with it
  - matp: sunshine's summary now gives both papers; the owner chose (2026-10-15) to take
    sun out of every frequency table (the general table and the tennessee_walking pool;
    its shares moved to N) since no exact figure per breed exists. A Lusitano table is
    worked out from Cr 0.42 and prl 0.064 (the only breed with both given).
  - dun, extension, tiger_eye: nothing new can be tabled. The Percheron e 0.12 has no
    filtered ea beside it; PRE's D is still not stated.

QUESTION C6 - KIT AND MITF FREQUENCIES BY BREED; SW10 IN PRE (2026-10-15)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Her answer, as she sent it (tables reflowed for plain text):

Short answers
  - Avila et al. 2022 did not test W13, W32, W34 or W35. Among KIT variants it tested only
    SB1, W5, W10, W20, W22 and tobiano (TO), so an "all KIT variants combined" total
    cannot be calculated from it. (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9498372/)
  - The W20 allele frequency counts every W20 mutation the W20 assay detected, including
    one on a chromosome that also carries W22. But the study did not phase W20/W22
    compound heterozygotes or report a W20_22 haplotype, so it cannot say how many W20
    alleles were on W22 backgrounds. The three Quarter Horses called W20/W22 could be cis
    (W20_22) or trans.
  - McFadden et al. 2023 did not report a breed-wide SW10 allele frequency for PRE. A
    later review says SW10 had been seen in two PRE horses: a count, not a frequency.
    (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37406837/)

Avila et al. 2022: 11,281 horses in 28 breeds from UC Davis VGL test results; 6,878 after
removing first-degree relatives. All figures are allele frequencies after relatedness
filtering. KIT assays: SB1, W5, W10, W20, W22 (Table 2), TO (Supplementary Tables S3-S4);
W13, W32, W34, W35 not in the 29-variant panel.

W5, W10, W20, W22 by breed (Table 2), and their sum (a sum of allele frequencies, not a
share of carriers; SB1 and TO excluded, as the supplement's per-breed SB1 could not be
retrieved):
  Andalusian 0, 0, 0.011, 0 (0.011); Appaloosa 0, 0, 0.18, 0; Arabian all 0; Connemara
  Pony 0, 0, 0.12, 0; Dutch Warmblood 0, 0, 0.26, 0; Gypsy Cob 0, 0, 0.33, 0; Gypsy
  Vanner 0, 0, 0.27, 0; Hanoverian 0, 0, 0.24, 0; Icelandic Horse all 0; Knabstrupper 0,
  0, 0.18, 0; Lusitano 0, 0, 0.049, 0; Miniature Horse 0, 0, 0.046, 0; Missouri Fox
  Trotter 0, 0, 0.12, 0; Morgan Horse 0, 0, 0.092, 0; Mustang 0, 0, 0.20, 0; Norwegian
  Fjord all 0; Oldenburg 0, 0, 0.26, 0.015 (0.275); Paint Horse 0, 0, 0.21, 0.0015
  (0.2115); Percheron 0, 0, 0.027, 0; Pony of the Americas 0, 0, 0.21, 0; Pura Raza
  Espanola 0, 0, 0.025, 0; Quarter Horse 0, 0.00031, 0.14, 0.00047 (0.14078); Rocky
  Mountain Horse 0, 0, 0.0087, 0; Shetland Pony 0, 0, 0.013, 0; Standardbred 0, 0, 0.026,
  0; Tennessee Walking Horse 0, 0, 0.072, 0; Thoroughbred 0.0075, 0, 0.34, 0.015
  (0.3625); Welsh Pony 0, 0, 0.26, 0.

SB1 and tobiano: SB1 in 171 horses of 10 breeds (Gypsy Cob, Gypsy Vanner, Miniature
Horse, Missouri Fox Trotter, Mustang, Paint Horse, Pony of the Americas, Quarter Horse,
Shetland Pony, Tennessee Walking Horse); highest 0.083 (Tennessee Walking), lowest 0.0010
(Quarter Horse); per-breed values in Supplementary Table S4, not retrieved. TO in 787
horses of 15 breeds, 0.0033 (Arabian) to 0.22 (Shetland Pony).

W20 and W20_22: W20 in 25 of 28 breeds; 340 W20/W20 homozygotes in the unfiltered data.
The frequency is from the W20 SNP assay, so counts W20 alleles whatever else is on the
chromosome. Three W20/W22 Quarter Horses; genotype calls cannot tell cis from trans. Its
advice: treat W20 and W22 as separate unless phased; do not assume W22 implies W20.

MITF in Avila et al.: SW1, SW3, SW5 and SW6 tested; not SW7, SW8 or SW9. SW2 and SW4 are
PAX3. By breed (SW1, SW3, SW5, SW6):
  Appaloosa 0.035; Dutch Warmblood 0.018; Gypsy Cob 0.026; Icelandic Horse 0.076;
  Knabstrupper 0.036; Miniature Horse 0.10; Morgan Horse 0.018; Oldenburg 0.016; Paint
  Horse 0.053, 0.00056, 0.0022, 0.00056; Pony of the Americas 0.014; Quarter Horse 0.017,
  0.00036, 0, 0; Shetland Pony 0.0070; Welsh Pony 0.057 (others 0).
  SW4 absent; SW2 only in Paint and Quarter Horse; no SW3, SW4, SW5 or SW6 homozygotes.

SW10 in PRE: McFadden et al. identified SW10 as a nonsense PAX3 variant,
XM_005610643.3:c.927C>T at ECA6:11,196,181 (EquCab3.0); OMIA gives
NC_009149.3:g.11196181G>A (https://omia.org/OMIA001688/9796/). The abstract reports an
association with more depigmentation in PRE horses, N = 30, but no breed frequency. A
2024 review (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10854722/) says SW10 had been seen
in two PRE horses. Journal of Equine Veterinary Science 128:104875,
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jevs.2023.104875

What the data does with it
  - kit: sixteen breed tables, for the breeds where W20 is the only tested variant found
    (no SB1, W5, W10 or W22): Andalusian, Appaloosa, Arabian, Connemara Pony, Dutch
    Warmblood, Hanoverian, Icelandic Horse, Knabstrupper, Lusitano, Morgan Horse,
    Norwegian Fjord, Percheron, PRE, Rocky Mountain Horse, Standardbred, Welsh Pony.
    Worked out assuming random mating; untested variants count as N. Breeds with SB1 (no
    per-breed figure) or W22 (W20_22 cannot be told apart) have none.
  - mitf: thirteen breed tables, the breeds listed; SW7-SW9 count as N.
  - pax3: no table; SW10's OMIA position noted.
