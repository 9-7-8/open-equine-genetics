FIRST ANSWER: MATP / SLC45A2, the Cream–Pearl locus in horses
-------------------------------------------------------------

MATP is the older name for the equine SLC45A2 gene, the locus behind Cream dilution and Pearl dilution. It alters pigment processing in melanocytes but does not create a new base color; it dilutes the red and/or black pigment produced by the horse’s underlying MC1R/ASIP base coat.[[vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/cream)][[vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/pearl)][[omia](https://www.omia.org/OMIA001344/9796/)]

Gene identity
-------------

MATP stands for membrane-associated transporter protein. The gene is now formally called SLC45A2 (solute carrier family 45 member 2), and it lies on equine chromosome 21. UC Davis identifies the cream mutation as SLC45A2 c.457G>A in exon 2; the pearl mutation is SLC45A2 c.985G>A in exon 4. [13][16][14]

SLC45A2 is thought to help traffic pigment-related molecules to developing melanocytes. When that transport is disrupted, melanin is produced less effectively or distributed differently, producing diluted hair, skin, and eye pigment. [13][16]

Established alleles
-------------------

Allele | Common notation | Inheritance | Effect
Wild type | N, n, or C | Normal | No MATP/SLC45A2 dilution
Cream | Cr | Incomplete dominant | One copy dilutes red strongly and black minimally; two copies dilute both red and black strongly. [13]
Pearl | Prl | Recessive alone | One copy usually has no visible coat effect; two copies dilute coat, mane, tail, and skin. [16]
Cream + Pearl compound heterozygote | Cr/Prl | Compound heterozygous | Produces a pseudo-double-cream phenotype resembling a double dilute, often with pale skin and blue/green eyes. [16]

UC Davis currently lists N and Cr for its Cream test and N and Prl for its Pearl test. [13][16]

Effect on red pigment
---------------------

Cream has a strong, dosage-dependent effect on phaeomelanin—the reddish/yellow pigment in chestnut-based coats. [13]

Base coat | One Cream allele | Two Cream alleles
Chestnut | Palomino: gold body, flaxen or pale mane/tail | Cremello: pale cream body, mane, and tail; pink skin; blue eyes. [13]
Bay | Buckskin: tan/gold body with black points largely retained | Perlino: very pale body with diluted points. [13]
Black | Smoky black: often black or nearly black; cream may be subtle or invisible | Smoky cream: pale cream double dilute. [13]

A single Cream allele dilutes red to yellow or gold, while its effect on black pigment is usually subtle. Two Cream alleles dilute both pigment types substantially, although black-based double dilutes may retain somewhat more pigment than chestnut-based double dilutes. [13]

Effect on black pigment
-----------------------

Cream’s effect on eumelanin—black pigment—is weak in single dose. A bay with one Cream copy usually remains clearly buckskin because the black mane, tail, and lower legs remain dark; a black horse with one Cream copy may be called smoky black and can be difficult or impossible to identify visually. [13]

With two Cream alleles, black pigment is strongly diluted. On a bay base this gives perlino; on a black base it gives smoky cream. [13]

Pearl, by contrast, is not visibly expressed in one copy on black, bay, or chestnut bases. In two copies, it dilutes both red and black pigment, and on chestnut it creates a pale, uniform apricot body, mane, and tail with pale skin. [16]

Cream × Pearl interaction
-------------------------

A horse with one Cream allele and one Pearl allele is a compound heterozygote: it carries two different dilution variants at the same SLC45A2 locus. This combination can mimic a true double cream, producing a pale coat, pale skin, and blue or green eyes; UC Davis calls these “pseudo-double cream” phenotypes. [16]

This matters for breeding because Cr/Prl is not Cr/Cr. The foal may look like a double dilute, but it carries only one Cream allele and one Pearl allele rather than two Cream alleles. [16]

Genotypes and expected phenotypes
---------------------------------

SLC45A2 genotype | Typical visible result
N/N | No Cream or Pearl dilution from this locus
N/Cr | Palomino, buckskin, or smoky black, depending on base color
Cr/Cr | Cremello, perlino, or smoky cream, depending on base color
N/Prl | No visible Pearl effect; carrier
Prl/Prl | Pearl dilution, often apricot on chestnut base
Cr/Prl | Pseudo-double-cream phenotype; pale coat, skin, and often blue/green eyes

The phenotype also depends on the base color genes MC1R and ASIP, plus any additional dilutions or white patterns. Cream does not override chestnut/bay/black genetics; it modifies the pigment those genes produce. [15][13]

What MATP does not do
---------------------

There is no well-established evidence that MATP/SLC45A2 determines height, speed, muscle mass, bone structure, hoof shape, feathering, or other structural traits. Its documented role is pigment dilution: hair, skin, and eye color. [13][16]

A few practical distinctions:

- Mane and tail color: Cream changes their color as part of the coat—for example, a palomino’s light mane and tail, or the pale mane and tail of a cremello or pearl. It does not independently control mane/tail length or texture. [13][16]
- Eye color: Cream and Cream/Pearl can produce blue or pale eyes; this is a pigment effect, not a vision or conformation trait. [13][16]
- Skin: Double cream and Pearl phenotypes have pale skin, but this is not the same as a white-spotting gene or a health condition caused by the dilution itself. [13][16]
- White markings: Genes such as those causing sabino, frame overo, tobiano, or dominant white are separate from SLC45A2. Cream can make a coat look near-white, but it is not a white-spotting allele. [13]

Breeds where it is common
-------------------------

Cream is appropriate for testing in many breeds and is widespread wherever palominos, buckskins, cremellos, perlinos, and smoky blacks are bred. UC Davis notes that its Cream test applies to “many breeds.” [13]

Pearl is much rarer. UC Davis specifically identifies it in:

- Andalusians
- Lusitanos
- Quarter Horses
- Paint Horses [16]

In Quarter Horses and Paints, Pearl was historically called Barlink Factor. UC Davis concludes that the same mutation occurs in Spanish breeds and Quarter Horse/Paint populations, likely reflecting Spanish ancestry in the latter breeds; therefore “Pearl” is the preferred name. [16]

Speculation and uncertainty
---------------------------

The core Cream and Pearl mutations are well established, but the locus still has open questions:

- Additional SLC45A2 variants: Research has identified Pearl as a separate missense variant in the same gene as Cream, and scientific discussion notes that multiple SLC45A2 variants can produce dilution phenotypes. This makes it plausible that additional rare alleles or modifiers exist, but they should not be assumed to exist without validation. [11][14]
- “Sunshine” and other named phenotypes: Some dilution-like phenotypes have been investigated in connection with SLC45A2 compound heterozygotes. Their inheritance and exact molecular classification are less settled than Cream and Pearl, so simulator designs should treat them as research-in-progress rather than confirmed standard alleles. [23]
- Phenotype overlap: Cream, Pearl, Champagne, Dun, Silver, and mushroom can resemble one another in some combinations. UC Davis explicitly cautions that its Cream test detects only the exon-2 Cream mutation and will not detect other dilution genes or mutations that resemble Cream. [13]
- Exact visual boundaries: A single Cream allele’s slight effect on black pigment can be inconsistent and hard to see, especially on smoky black. Coat-color naming is therefore phenotype-based and can be ambiguous without genetic testing. [13]

Future science
--------------

Likely future work includes searching for additional functional SLC45A2 variants, refining genotype-to-phenotype prediction in compound dilution horses, and distinguishing true double Cream horses from Cream/Pearl compound heterozygotes. The 2019 Pearl study provided direct evidence for the exon-4 mutation and clarified why Cream/Pearl horses resemble double dilutes. [11][14]

For a breeding simulator, the most defensible model is:

- Treat SLC45A2 as one locus with at least N, Cr, and Prl.
- Use incomplete dominance for Cr.
- Use recessive expression for Prl/Prl.
- Add a special Cr/Prl phenotype rule.
- Keep all structural traits—height, speed, muscle, bone, hooves, feathering—on separate genetic systems unless future evidence shows a link.

Key sources
-----------

- UC Davis Veterinary Genetics Laboratory, Cream test: alleles, inheritance, genotypes, and pigment effects. [13]
- UC Davis Veterinary Genetics Laboratory, Pearl test: Pearl inheritance, breeds, and Cream/Pearl interaction. [16]
- Mariat et al., 2003: original identification of MATP/SLC45A2 as the Cream locus. [18]
- Sevane et al., 2019: evidence that SLC45A2 exon 4 causes Pearl. [11]
- OMIA: consolidated record of MATP/SLC45A2, Cream, and Pearl variants. [14]

Quick simulator rule
For your horse-genetics project, implement MATP/SLC45A2 as a single locus with alleles N, Cr, and Prl:

* N/Cr → single cream
* Cr/Cr → double cream
* N/Prl → invisible carrier
* Prl/Prl → pearl
* Cr/Prl → pseudo-double cream

Then apply the result to the MC1R/ASIP-derived base color, rather than treating Cream as a base color itself.[[vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/cream)][[vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/pearl)][[vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/resources/horse-coat-color)]

SECOND ANSWER: snowdrop and sunshine
------------------------------------

Yes—you’re thinking of “snowdrop,” not “low snow.” Gypsy horses have a proposed third MATP/SLC45A2 dilution allele called snowdrop (C^sno), and a separate proposed variant called sunshine (C^sun) has also been associated with SLC45A2. But their evidence strength and current test availability differ substantially. [biorxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/851246v1)

The three proposed non-standard alleles
---------------------------------------

Proposed allele | Main breed association | Inheritance as reported | Current status
Snowdrop (C^sno) | Gypsy horses / Gypsy Vanners | Recessive; C^sno/C^sno dilutes both red and black; carrier reportedly no visible effect | Published as a novel SLC45A2 missense variant, but not a routine UC Davis commercial test.  [biorxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/851246v1)
Sunshine (C^sun) | Initially reported in compound-heterozygote dilute horses | Proposed recessive or compound-heterozygote effect | Its causality is now uncertain: a 2024 survey found the variant extremely common in many breeds, including horses without the reported dilution phenotype.  [visualize.jove](https://visualize.jove.com/31006892-a-candidate-gene-approach-identifies-variants-in-slc45a2-that-explain-dilute-phenotypes-pearl-and-sunshine-in-compound-heterozygote-horses)
Pearl (Prl) | Andalusians, Lusitanos, Quarter Horses, Paints; also occurs in Gypsy-related populations | Recessive alone; visible with Prl/Prl or Cr/Prl | Well established and commercially testable through UC Davis.

Snowdrop in Gypsy Vanners
-------------------------

The snowdrop report sequenced a dilute Gypsy horse that was negative for known dilution genotypes and identified SLC45A2:c.305G>A, named snowdrop (C^sno). In the reported homozygous horse, it produced a pale phenotype similar to homozygous Cream, diluting both red and black pigment; relatives carrying one copy reportedly showed no visible effect. [biorxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/851246v1)

So for a Gypsy Vanner, the plausible MATP allele set is:

- N — no MATP dilution
- Cr — Cream
- Prl — Pearl
- C^sno — Snowdrop, proposed/unconfirmed commercially

It would be premature to treat snowdrop as fully validated in the same sense as Cream or Pearl, because the original evidence came from a small Gypsy-horse sample and UC Davis does not currently list it among its standard equine color tests. [biorxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/851246v1)

Sunshine is more doubtful
-------------------------

Sunshine was proposed when researchers studied “false double dilutes”—horses that looked homozygous Cream but carried only one detectable Cream allele. The study reported a new C^sun variant that might explain some recessive or compound-heterozygote dilution phenotypes. [visualize.jove](https://visualize.jove.com/31006892-a-candidate-gene-approach-identifies-variants-in-slc45a2-that-explain-dilute-phenotypes-pearl-and-sunshine-in-compound-heterozygote-horses)

However, later population data found the sunshine-associated variant at very high frequency across many breeds—over 0.86 in Arabians, Belgians, Morgans, Thoroughbreds, and Quarter Horses—and concluded that more work is needed to determine whether it actually reduces pigmentation. That high frequency is inconsistent with a simple rare recessive dilution allele. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11006912/)

Practical takeaway: do not add C^sun as a confirmed MATP allele in a simulator yet. If you include it, mark it as speculative or as a modifier with unresolved causality. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11006912/)

What this means for your model
------------------------------

A defensible current model is:

  SLC45A2 / MATP locus:
  N        normal
  Cr       Cream — confirmed, incomplete dominant
  Prl      Pearl — confirmed, recessive; Cr/Prl pseudo-double cream
  C^sno    Snowdrop — proposed Gypsy-horse recessive dilution
  C^sun    Sunshine — proposed, causality currently uncertain

For gameplay, I’d implement Cr and Prl as fully deterministic, add C^sno as a rare Gypsy-lineage allele with recessive expression, and either omit C^sun or implement it behind a “research/disputed” flag. The key distinction is that snowdrop is a real published Gypsy-associated candidate allele, while sunshine is a candidate whose link to dilution is now questioned. [biorxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/851246v1)

THIRD ANSWER: every proposed allele
-----------------------------------

Yes—beyond the well-established Cream (Cr) and Pearl (Prl) alleles, the main additional proposed SLC45A2/MATP alleles are Sunshine (C^sun) and Snowdrop (C^sno). I would not treat either as being on the same evidentiary level as Cream or Pearl, and I found no credible evidence for additional named equine SLC45A2 dilution alleles beyond these. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31006892/)

Proposed allele inventory
-------------------------

Allele | Variant | First proposed | Claimed inheritance | Evidence status
Cream (Cr) | SLC45A2:c.457G>A | 2003 | Incomplete dominant | Confirmed; UC Davis offers testing.
Pearl (Prl) | SLC45A2:c.985G>A, p.Ala329Thr | 2019 | Recessive; Cr/Prl gives pseudo-double cream | Confirmed; UC Davis offers testing.  [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31006892/)
Sunshine (C^sun) | SLC45A2:c.568G>A, p.Gly190Arg | 2019 | Proposed recessive, pearl-like | Disputed / unresolved; originally proposed from a “false double dilute,” but later population data found the variant too common to fit that model cleanly.  [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31006892/)
Snowdrop (C^sno) | SLC45A2:c.305G>A, p.Arg102Gln | 2020 | Recessive; two copies dilute red and black | Published candidate, especially in Gypsy/Tinker/Irish Cob horses; commercial tests exist, but it remains much less broadly validated than Cream and Pearl.  [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31961951/)

Sunshine: proposed but shaky
----------------------------

Sunshine was named after the horse in which it was first investigated. The 2019 study found c.568G>A in a horse that looked like a double Cream but did not have two Cream alleles, leading the authors to propose C^sun as another recessive SLC45A2 dilution allele. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31006892/)

The problem is frequency: a 2024 review/population study reported the sunshine variant as extremely common in several breeds—over 86% allele frequency in Arabians, Belgians, Morgans, Thoroughbreds, and Quarter Horses—while many carriers showed no dilution. That makes a simple “sunshine is a recessive dilution allele” explanation difficult to support. 

So, for your simulator: do not make C^sun a confirmed recessive dilution allele. If you include it, use a “candidate/disputed” status or omit it. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31006892/)

Snowdrop: stronger, but still a candidate
-----------------------------------------

Snowdrop was identified in a Gypsy-breed mare that looked cremello-like but tested negative for Cream, Pearl, and major white-spotting variants. The reported variant is SLC45A2:c.305G>A; the authors proposed the name snowdrop (C^sno) and described it as autosomal recessive. [omia](https://omia.org/OMIA001344/9796/)

In the reported homozygote, it dilutes both red and black pigment, producing a very pale, Cream-like coat. One copy is generally described as a silent carrier. [laboklin](https://laboklin.com/en/products/genetics/coat-colour-coat-structure-coat-length/horse/snowdrop/)

Unlike sunshine, snowdrop has a more coherent rare-recessive story and is now offered by at least some commercial laboratories. Still, the original publication was based on a very small number of affected horses, and UC Davis does not appear to list Snowdrop among its standard equine coat-color tests. That makes it a well-supported candidate, not a fully settled allele like Cream or Pearl. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31961951/)

Are there more?
---------------

I did not find credible published proposals for additional named equine SLC45A2 dilution alleles beyond Cream, Pearl, Sunshine, and Snowdrop. The literature instead emphasizes that multiple variants at this one gene can produce dilution-like phenotypes, and that some “false double dilutes” may still have unexplained genetics. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31006892/)

A few important non-alleles to keep separate:

- Mushroom is not SLC45A2; it is associated with a different dilution mechanism and is mainly studied in Shetlands and related breeds.
- Champagne, Dun, and Silver are separate dilution loci, not MATP alleles. [docta.ucm](https://docta.ucm.es/bitstreams/460e9be0-d9bb-4e69-b222-4bc15e526aa5/download)
- “Pseudo-double cream” is a phenotype, not an allele; it can result from Cr/Prl, potentially C^sno combinations, or other unresolved genetics. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31006892/)

Simulator recommendation
------------------------

Use this tiered implementation:

  Confirmed:
  N, Cr, Prl
  
  Strong candidate:
  C^sno  — recessive; Gypsy/Tinker/Irish Cob association
  
  Disputed:
  C^sun  — do not implement as a confirmed dilution allele

For inheritance, I’d model Cr as incomplete dominant, while Prl/Prl and C^sno/C^sno are recessive dilutions. The uncertain part is compound genotypes: Cr/Prl is well documented, but interactions such as Cr/C^sno, Prl/C^sno, or C^sno/C^sun are not sufficiently characterized to hard-code precise phenotypes. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31006892/)

One gene, four dilution alleles
-------------------------------

MATP - now usually called SLC45A2 - is a pigmentation gene and nothing else. The locus is multiallelic: a horse has two copies, and each may be normal N, Cream, Pearl, Sunshine, Snowdrop, or something not yet found. Cream and Pearl are not separate genes, and neither are the two newer ones.

Gene | SLC45A2, older name MATP; also historically discussed under "underwhite" / OCA4 nomenclature
Chromosome | ECA21
Cream | c.457G>A, p.Asp153Asn (D153N), exon 2 - incompletely dominant, dosage-dependent
Pearl | c.985G>A, p.Ala329Thr (A329T), exon 4 - recessive, with an important interaction with Cream
Sunshine | c.568G>A, p.Gly190Arg - missense, reported 2019, recessive
Snowdrop | c.305G>A, p.Arg102Gln - missense, reported 2020, recessive
Disease status | None established for any of the four

So "palomino", "buckskin", "cremello", "perlino" and "pearl" are phenotype labels arising from combinations of this locus with a base colour - not names of single genes. Which is exactly the structure the mod implements: five alleles at one locus, and outcomes read off the pair.

Cream, which is the one with a dosage effect
--------------------------------------------

genotype | chestnut base | bay base | black base
N/N | chestnut | bay | black
N/Cr | palomino | buckskin | smoky black, often subtle
Cr/Cr | cremello | perlino | smoky cream

One Cr most visibly dilutes pheomelanin. A buckskin keeps dark mane, tail, legs and ear edges because those are eumelanin - and the reason is not that cream "fails" on black pigment, but that at one dose its visual effect on eumelanin is much less obvious than on red. Smoky black can be so subtle that it passes for an ordinary dark, sun-faded or seal-brown horse without a test.

Two copies dilute both pigments far more. Double-cream horses characteristically have pale pink or rosy skin and pale blue eyes, and are frequently miscalled "white" - they are dilute-coloured horses, not genetically white-patterned ones. Their pale hair is hair with reduced pigment, not an absence of pigment cells the way most white spotting works. Perlino often keeps a warmer, peachier cast in mane, tail and lower legs; smoky cream can read warmer or darker than a typical cremello from residual pigment. UC Davis, cream (https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/cream)

Pearl, and the compound heterozygote that fools everyone
--------------------------------------------------------

Pearl is autosomal recessive: N/prl is an outwardly non-pearl carrier, prl/prl shows the dilution, and Cr/prl shows a strong dilution because the horse has no normal allele at the locus at all. It is also called apricot, and historically the Barlink Factor in Quarter Horse colour discussions.

Homozygous pearl gives a diluted body, mane and tail with pale skin; on chestnut, a relatively uniform pale apricot. On bay and black backgrounds the outcome varies more, because base colour and unidentified modifiers change how much residual dark pigment shows.

Cr/prl is the famous one: very pale coat, pale or pinkish skin, blue or blue-green eyes - a pseudo-double-dilute that may be impossible to distinguish from cremello, perlino or smoky cream without DNA testing, despite carrying only one cream allele. UC Davis, pearl (https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/pearl)

The mod gets this row exactly right, and gives it its own eye
Cr/prl resolves to double-dilute in the mod, the same outcome as Cr/Cr, which is what the literature describes. Where the mod goes a step further is the eye: Cr/Cr gets the cremello blue, prl/prl a light bright gold, and Cr/prl a green - a deliberate reading of "blue or blue-green eyes" that makes the compound heterozygote identifiable in-game where a real one is not identifiable by eye at all. That is a divergence in the player's favour rather than an error, and it is worth knowing which way round it goes.

Sunshine and Snowdrop
---------------------

Both are newer, rarer, and much thinner on evidence than Cream and Pearl. Both behave primarily as recessive dilutions: a carrier looks non-dilute, and two dilution alleles at the locus can give a strongly pale, double-dilute-like phenotype.

Sunshine
--------

Reported in 2019 after investigation of a horse that looked like a double-cream dilute but tested as carrying only one cream allele. Glycine 190 becomes arginine - small and flexible replaced by large and positively charged, plausibly disruptive, though that is an inference from the substitution rather than a demonstrated mechanism. The allele is named Csun after Captain Sunshine, the horse in which it was recognised; he was pale cream in coat, mane and tail with blue eyes, and was Cr/sun, not Cr/Cr. The study also found the allele heterozygous in one non-dilute offspring, supporting a single copy being visually silent. Anim. Genet. 10.1111/age.12790 (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/age.12790)

genotype | expected
N/sun | carrier; no clear phenotype known
sun/sun | expected recessive dilution - phenotype not well documented
Cr/sun | strong dilution; pseudo-double-dilute, documented
prl/sun, sno/sun | plausible compound dilutes; insufficient published data

The biggest gap is sun/sun. There is no robust body of documented homozygotes defining the chestnut, bay or black phenotype, typical skin and eye colour, residual point pigment, or whether expression is as consistent as prl/prl or Cr/Cr. A 2026 breed-colour report described a second Cr/sun horse, a Tennessee Walking Horse colt from a carrier dam - useful evidence the allele is not confined to one family, and an emerging observation rather than a population study.

Snowdrop
--------

Reported in 2020 in a Gypsy horse with a very dilute coat that carried none of the then-known explanations. Arginine 102 becomes glutamine; the allele is Csno. The reported sno/sno horse showed strong dilution of both red and black pigment and looked like a homozygous cream. In the discovery pedigree, both sire and dam carried one copy with no observable effect. bioRxiv 851246 (https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/851246v1)

Snowdrop is the clearest evidence that this locus should be understood as a series of functional dilution alleles rather than a two-allele normal-versus-cream system. Cr/sno, prl/sno and sun/sno are all uncharacterised in published evidence. It is tempting to assume any two non-normal alleles here give a strong phenotype - a reasonable hypothesis given the Cream/Pearl and Cream/Sunshine observations, and not a settled fact.

allele | one copy with N | two copies | compound with cream | evidence
Cream | visible palomino / buckskin / smoky black | cremello, perlino, smoky cream | - | extensive
Pearl | visually silent carrier | pearl dilution | pseudo-double dilute | strong
Sunshine | no clear effect reported | expected, poorly documented | pseudo-double dilute, documented | limited
Snowdrop | no clear effect reported | strong dilute, double-cream-like | not characterised | limited

Cream is the odd one out because one copy shows. The other three are best treated as recessive or recessive-like functional alleles: a normal allele preserves enough SLC45A2 activity that the carrier is not visibly diluted, while two altered copies substantially reduce pigmentation.

The mod fills in what the literature leaves blank
The four-allele structure and the recessive behaviour of pearl, sunshine and snowdrop are all faithful. What the mod has to do that the science cannot is answer every cell of the table - sun/sun, Cr/sno, prl/sun and the rest - because a genotype in a game has to render as something. Those rows are the mod's extrapolation from the Cream/Pearl pattern, which is the same reasonable hypothesis the source flags as unproven. They are not wrong; they are simply beyond what anyone has observed.

Skin and eyes
-------------

Skin is dose- and genotype-dependent: single-cream horses usually keep dark skin under coloured areas, with pink skin under ordinary white markings; double-cream horses commonly have pink or rosy skin; homozygous pearl shows paler skin; and Cr/prl commonly has pale or pinkish skin. Pink skin is not disease and does not mean a lethal-white foal - it means little visible melanin at that location.

Eyes. A single cream allele generally has little obvious effect. Double-cream horses commonly have blue eyes; Cr/prl can have blue or blue-green; pearl homozygotes may have lighter eyes, less uniformly described. A light eye alone cannot diagnose the genotype - blue eyes also come from several white-pattern genes, notably splashed white. The mod handles that collision the same way, by ranking the eye contributions rather than letting the last one win.

Health
------

Current references treat cream and pearl as coat-colour dilutions, not disease mutations. The breed-distribution study notes that some pigment variants do have medically important pleiotropic effects - and names silver/MCOA, frame overo/lethal white, and leopard complex/CSNB as the examples. It identifies no comparable association for cream or pearl.

So: no established lethal effect, no developmental disorder, no proven eye malformation association, no CSNB association, and no adverse syndrome tied to Cr/Cr, prl/prl or Cr/prl. The same holds for sunshine and snowdrop, with the caveat that being very rare and recently recognised is not the same as having every subtle effect excluded.

locus | phenotype | associated concern
SLC45A2 | cream, pearl, sunshine, snowdrop | no confirmed inherited disease
PMEL | silver | multiple congenital ocular anomalies, worse in homozygotes
EDNRB | frame overo | homozygous lethal white overo syndrome
TRPM1 | leopard complex | congenital stationary night blindness in homozygotes
STX17 | grey | increased melanoma risk with age

"Dilution" is a visual category, not a biological or health one. The one real management point is that pale skin has less natural UV screening - shade, UV fly masks, sun sheets - which follows from pigmentation generally and is not a disease phenotype of these alleles.

Build, size, jumping, speed
---------------------------

None are known to be caused by any allele at this locus. No credible evidence links cream or pearl to withers height, bone circumference, head shape, muscling, frame, body mass, growth rate, adult size, hoof quality or structural soundness; nor to jumping scope, technique, carefulness, bascule, tendon durability, trainability or competition results; nor to sprint speed, race performance, stride length, aerobic capacity, fibre profile, recovery or gaitedness. The best-known single-gene association with alternate gaits is DMRT3.

Where colour looks associated with performance in a real population, the candidates are breed structure (cream is common in some populations and absent from others, and breeds differ enormously in what they were selected for), founder effects (a notable stallion carrying both a colour allele and athletic traits), breeder selection for colour and type together, and plain visual bias - pale horses can look larger or finer-boned depending on light and contrast.

Breeds and frequency
--------------------

Cream is widespread. In the UC Davis dataset of 11,281 horses across 28 breeds it was detected in 25 of the 28. In the relatedness-filtered sample its frequency ran from 0.42 in Lusitanos down to 0.013 in Standardbreds, and it was not detected in the sampled Hanoverian, Knabstrupper or Percheron groups - absent from those samples, not proven absent from those breeds. PMC9498372 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9498372/)

Pearl is much more restricted, with a strong Iberian distribution consistent with where it was first identified. It appeared in 7 of the 28 breeds:

breed | pearl allele frequency
Andalusian | 12%
Pura Raza Española | 11%
Lusitano | 6.4%
Gypsy Cob | 1.7%
Gypsy Vanner | 1.5%
American Paint Horse | 0.7%
American Quarter Horse | 0.031%

Those are allele frequencies, not percentages of visibly pearl horses - and since pearl needs prl/prl or Cr/prl to be conspicuous, visible pearls are far rarer than carriers. The tiny Quarter Horse figure fits the historical Barlink Factor label: it exists and is uncommon. The 11-12% in Iberians makes pearl a live breeding consideration there.

Sunshine and Snowdrop have no population frequency at all. Sunshine was first identified in a horse of Standardbred x Tennessee Walking Horse ancestry, with a later Tennessee Walking Horse colt and carrier dam - enough to make that ancestry worth testing, and not enough to say the allele "belongs" to either breed. No peer-reviewed survey has established a frequency, an origin breed, a founder, a geographic distribution, or whether it is genuinely rare or merely rarely tested. Snowdrop is known in Gypsy horse / Cob / Tinker ancestry, with laboratory guidance noting that presence elsewhere has not been described and cannot be excluded. "Rare" is a fair description and not a measured percentage.

How common each allele is in a wild horse
-----------------------------------------

The founder numbers follow the one large survey there is: 11,281 horses from 28 breeds ("Breed Distribution and Allele Frequencies of Base Coat Color, Dilution, and White Patterning Variants across 28 Horse Breeds", 2022 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9498372/)). Cream turned up in 25 of the 28 breeds, at allele frequencies from 0.013 in Standardbreds up to breeds bred for it, so one copy in 30 (0.033) is a fair middle for a mixed wild population. Pearl turned up in only seven: the Iberian breeds carry it at 0.06 to 0.12 (Andalusian 0.12, Pura Raza Española 0.11, Lusitano 0.064), and everywhere else it is scarce (Gypsy Cob 0.017, Gypsy Vanner 0.015, Paint 0.007, Quarter Horse 0.0003). A mixed population is mostly not Iberian, so the mod founds pearl at one copy in 300, about a tenth as common as cream. Sunshine and snowdrop have no measured frequency (above), so they sit an order of magnitude below pearl again, at one in 3,000 and one in 3,500: rare enough that a homozygote is bred, not met.

Until 2026-09-15 pearl was one copy in 22, which made a founder horse likelier to carry pearl than cream, the reverse of real horses. The number dated from when cream and pearl were two separate genes, each tuned alone, and the comparison was not revisited when they became one locus. These numbers only reach Feral Mixed horses; each breed rolls its own sheet, below.

What is settled, and what is not
--------------------------------

Strongly established. Cream and pearl are alleles of SLC45A2/MATP; cream is c.457G>A / p.Asp153Asn; pearl is c.985G>A / p.Ala329Thr; cream acts with a dosage-dependent, incomplete-dominance pattern; pearl is recessive alone and interacts visibly with cream in compound heterozygotes; both affect coat, skin and eye pigment; and there is no established direct effect on athletic ability, conformation or size.

Still uncertain. The full molecular mechanism - SLC45A2 clearly participates in melanosome pigmentation, and the exact chain from each equine variant to each visual phenotype is unresolved. Why horses with the same colour genotype vary in shade, mane and tail colour, residual point pigment and eye tone. Which additional genes govern shade, seasonal change and flaxen-like mane effects. Whether very subtle physiological effects of altered pigmentation exist below the threshold of detection - there is no evidence for any, so none should be assumed. And essentially everything about sun/sun and the snowdrop compounds.

The practical caution, and it is the one this locus exists to teach: visual colour names cannot replace genotype here. A pale blue-eyed horse can be Cr/Cr, Cr/prl, Cr/sun, sno/sno, or something at a different locus entirely.
