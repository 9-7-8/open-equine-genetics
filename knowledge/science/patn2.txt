# Second leopard-complex modifier besides PATN1

## Bottom line

**No second leopard-complex modifier comparable to PATN1 has been published.** “PATN2” is a useful simulation label for the remaining genetic variation in LP-pattern extent, but it is **not an identified gene, locus, variant, or validated test**. UC Davis states that additional modifiers are thought to explain the range of leopard-complex patterning, but only PATN1 has been molecularly associated. [web:165][web:166][web:186]

The best current model is therefore:

```text
LP determines whether leopard-complex traits occur.
PATN1 is the one proven major modifier of white-pattern extent.
PATN2 and further modifiers remain hypothetical/polygenic placeholders.
```

## Primary evidence

### LP itself

Leopard complex spotting (LP) is caused by an incompletely dominant 1,378 bp retroviral insertion in **TRPM1** on ECA1. It produces the LP-associated traits: varnish roaning, mottled skin, striped hooves, white sclera, and variable white patterning; LP/LP also causes congenital stationary night blindness. [web:166][web:188]

- Primary LP paper: Bellone et al. 2013, *PLoS ONE* 8:e78280, DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0078280](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0078280), PubMed: [24167615](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24167615/). [web:188]
- LP is necessary for the leopard-complex phenotype, but it does not by itself determine how much white a horse has. [web:165][web:166]

### PATN1

**Holl, H. M., Brooks, S. A., Archer, S., Brown, K., Malvick, J., Penedo, M. C. T., Bellone, R. R. (2016). “Variant in the *RFWD3* gene associated with PATN1, a modifier of leopard complex spotting.” *Animal Genetics* 47(1):91–101.**  
DOI: [10.1111/age.12375](https://doi.org/10.1111/age.12375)  
PubMed: [26568529](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26568529/) [web:186]

PATN1 is associated with a SNP in the 3′ UTR of *RFWD3*:

- SNP: **ECA3:23,658,447T>G**
- Assembly: reported in the paper’s ECA3 context; the exact reference-assembly version should be confirmed against the paper’s supplemental data before embedding a coordinate in a database.
- Association: \(p = 1.063 	imes 10^{-39}\) in fine mapping; \(p = 4.17 	imes 10^{-115}\) in the larger validation set.
- Effect: increases white patterning in horses that also carry LP.
- Inheritance: PATN1 behaves as a dominant modifier in the studied families. [web:186]

UC Davis reports that LP horses with PATN1 commonly have about **60–100% white**, while LP horses without PATN1 commonly have **0–40% white at birth**. [web:165]

## Status of “PATN2”

| Question | Status |
|---|---|
| Is PATN2 a published gene? | No. |
| Is PATN2 a mapped locus? | No. |
| Is there a PATN2 causal variant? | No. |
| Is there a PATN2 DNA test? | No. |
| Is there an official PATN2 allele symbol? | No. |
| Do additional LP modifiers exist? | Yes, inferred from unexplained variation in LP-pattern extent. [web:165][web:166] |
| Is “PATN2” scientifically established? | No; it is a placeholder/simulation convention. [web:172][web:186] |

No peer-reviewed paper has mapped a second major modifier or named a causal PATN2 variant. The OMIA entry for leopard complex lists LP/TRPM1 and PATN1/RFWD3, but no PATN2 entry or causal variant. [web:188][web:186]

## What the evidence supports

UC Davis explicitly says that “other genes” determine the amount of white patterning in LP horses and that PATN1 is one of those modifiers. [web:165][web:166]

That supports the existence of **additional genetic influence**, but not a specific second major locus. The remaining variation could be:

- one or more undiscovered major modifiers;
- several minor modifiers;
- polygenic variation;
- LP dosage effects;
- developmental stochasticity;
- breed-specific modifier haplotypes. [web:165][web:186][web:188]

## Simulator implementation

Use **PATN1 as the proven major modifier** and implement a second modifier only as a hidden, unlabeled polygenic component—or as a clearly fictional “PATN2” locus if your game needs a discrete inheritance system.

### Recommended realistic model

```text
if LP_present:
    pattern_extent =
        LP_dosage_effect
      + PATN1_dominant_effect
      + polygenic_modifier_effect
      + developmental_noise
else:
    pattern_extent = 0
```

Suggested values:

```text
LP/n  = varnish roaning, mottled skin, striped hooves, white sclera
LP/LP = stronger LP effects; CSNB; often less residual pigment

PATN1 present:
    LP/n  -> leopard or near-leopard tendency
    LP/LP -> few-spot or near-few-spot tendency

PATN1 absent:
    LP/n  -> 0–40% white tendency
    LP/LP -> variable, often less-extensive white pattern
```

### Optional PATN2 model

If you want a discrete second modifier:

```text
PATN2/PATN2 or PATN2/patn2 = additional white-pattern extent
patn2/patn2                = no additional effect
```

But label it **“PATN2 (simulation convention; not a published locus)”** in any genetics documentation. Do not give it a genomic coordinate, allele frequency, or DNA test. [web:172][web:186]

## Pattern-shape versus pattern-extent

PATN1 is primarily associated with **amount of white**, not with a specific blanket-versus-leopard “shape gene.” The older idea that PATN2 was specifically the “blanket gene” has been abandoned; Horse Reality explicitly notes that real-life PATN2 was once theorized to be the blanket gene but is not established. [web:172]

For a realistic simulator:

- Model **extent** as a continuous value.
- Model **distribution**—for example, hindquarter-concentrated blanket versus more generalized white—using separate developmental rules or additional hidden modifiers.
- Do not claim that PATN2 creates blankets while PATN1 creates leopards.

## Breeds and testing

PATN1 has been identified in LP-carrying breeds including **Appaloosa, British Spotted Pony, American Miniature Horse, Noriker, and Knabstrupper**, among others. [web:165]

UC Davis offers validated tests for **LP/TRPM1** and **PATN1/RFWD3**. It does not offer a PATN2 test. [web:165][web:166][web:168]

No reliable PATN2 allele frequency, carrier frequency, population sample size, or survey year can be reported, because no PATN2 genotype exists.

## Effects outside coat color

LP itself has a well-established non-coat effect: **LP/LP causes congenital stationary night blindness (CSNB)** due to the TRPM1 insertion. [web:166][web:188]

By contrast, there is **no evidence** that a second LP modifier affects height, speed, muscle, bone structure, hoof shape, feathering, mane/tail color, or health. Any PATN2 health effect would be unsupported speculation. [web:165][web:186]

## Future science

A real PATN2 discovery would require:

1. Large LP-positive cohorts with quantitative white-pattern scoring.
2. Family-based linkage or GWAS conditioned on LP and PATN1.
3. Whole-genome sequencing of high-white and low-white LP horses.
4. Replication across Appaloosas, Knabstruppers, Norikers, Miniature Horses, and spotted ponies.
5. Functional validation of any candidate regulatory variant.

Until then, the scientifically accurate answer is: **PATN1 is the only published major leopard-complex pattern modifier; PATN2 remains hypothetical.** [web:186][web:165][web:188]
