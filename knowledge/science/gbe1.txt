- GBE1 is on chromosome 26 (26q12-q13). The variant is c.102C>A in exon 1.
- UC Davis estimates 8.3% carriers in Quarter Horses and 7.1% in Paint Horses; older broad
  estimates of roughly 10% in Quarter Horse, Paint and related bloodlines also appear.
- Asked which 2006 study gave allele frequencies of 4.1% and 3.6%, the answer names "the
  2006 Ward et al. study" as the report of the mutation and gives no carrier percentages
  or address for it. The question is still open.
- Affected foals may be aborted, stillborn, weak at birth, or die within months.

GBE1 is not a coat-color locus. In horses, it is best known for glycogen branching enzyme deficiency (GBED), a fatal autosomal-recessive metabolic disease concentrated in Quarter Horse–related breeds. UC Davis lists the normal allele as N and the disease allele as G; a foal needs two copies of G to be affected. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)

Locus and function
------------------

GBE1 encodes glycogen branching enzyme, which creates branch points in glycogen—the body’s compact, rapidly mobilizable glucose-storage molecule. Without functional GBE1, tissues cannot form normally branched glycogen, so energy storage and release are severely impaired. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)

The equine disease allele is a single-base substitution, c.102C>A, in exon 1. It creates a premature stop codon, p.Tyr34Ter (Y34*), so no functional glycogen-branching enzyme is produced. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)

Alleles and inheritance
-----------------------

UC Davis genotype | Meaning | Breeding outcome
N/N | Normal; not a carrier | Cannot pass GBED to offspring.  [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)
N/G | Carrier; clinically unaffected | Passes G to about 50% of foals.  [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)
G/G | Affected | GBED is fatal; no viable adult affected horses.  [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)

Two carriers produce, on average, 25% affected, 50% carriers, and 25% N/N foals. Because affected foals die before breeding, the disease allele is maintained only through clinically normal carriers. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)

Clinical effects
----------------

GBED affects the fetus or newborn foal rather than producing a survivable adult phenotype. Reported outcomes include late-term abortion, stillbirth, weakness, seizures, muscle weakness, and early neonatal death; the abnormal glycogen storage especially compromises skeletal muscle, heart muscle, liver, and brain. [ceh.vetmed.ucdavis](https://ceh.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/health-topics/glycogen-branching-enzyme-deficiency-gbed)

Carrier horses (N/G) have roughly half normal GBE activity but are otherwise asymptomatic; there is no evidence that carrying one copy causes a visible coat, conformational, or performance phenotype. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7198616/)

What it does not affect
-----------------------

GBE1 is a metabolic enzyme gene, not a pigment, structural, or performance-selection locus. There are no well-established equine GBE1 effects on:

- Red versus black pigment, bay/black/chestnut expression, mane or tail color, or dilution/pattern phenotypes.
- Height, speed, muscle mass, bone structure, hoof shape, or feathering.
- Any adult-onset visible trait; homozygous G/G foals do not survive to display adult traits. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)

For a simulator, model GBE1 as a hidden health/lethality allele, not a visible trait: N/G is invisible, while G/G should result in abortion, stillbirth, or neonatal death rather than an adult horse.

Breeds and frequency
--------------------

GBED is found primarily in American Quarter Horses, American Paint Horses, and related Quarter Horse-lineage breeds; mixed-breed horses with Quarter Horse ancestry can also carry it. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)

A major 2006 frequency study found mutant-allele frequencies of about 4.1% in registered Quarter Horses, 3.6% in Paint Horses, and 0% in Thoroughbreds. Carrier percentages are therefore much higher than allele frequencies—roughly 8–9% in Quarter Horse and Paint populations in later summaries. [merckvetmanual](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/musculoskeletal-system/myopathies-in-horses/hereditary-and-congenital-myopathies-in-horses)

Established vs. speculative
---------------------------

Well established:

- Equine GBED is caused by GBE1 c.102C>A / p.Y34*. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)
- Inheritance is autosomal recessive. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)
- G/G is lethal in fetuses or neonates. [ceh.vetmed.ucdavis](https://ceh.vetmed.ucdavis.edu/health-topics/glycogen-branching-enzyme-deficiency-gbed)
- The mutation traces to a single founder and is especially relevant to Quarter Horse-related breeding programs. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17063718/)

Not established / should not be assumed:

- Additional pathogenic equine GBE1 alleles analogous to the many human GSD IV variants have not been established as routine equine disease alleles; the c.102C>A variant is the recognized equine test target. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)
- Claims that GBE1 carrier status changes athletic ability, muscle type, temperament, coat color, or conformation are speculation rather than validated phenotype associations. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)
- Gene-editing research has demonstrated correction of the mutation in equine cells, but this is experimental science, not an established breeding or clinical treatment. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7198616/)

Testing and breeding use
------------------------

UC Davis offers a direct DNA test reporting N/N, N/G, or G/G. The practical breeding rule is simple: avoid N/G × N/G matings, because each such cross has a 25% chance of an affected foal. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)

For a genetics simulation, use:

- Alleles: N (functional), G (GBED).
- Zygosity: N/N normal; N/G unaffected carrier; G/G embryonic/fetal or neonatal lethal.
- Visibility: none for carriers; no coat-color interaction.
- Population structure: founder effect in Quarter Horse-derived lines, with reduced but nonzero carrier frequency in related breeds and mixes. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/gbed)
