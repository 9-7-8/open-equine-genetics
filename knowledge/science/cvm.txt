CVM (cervical malformation) - science notes
(A science note on this locus, written 2026-10-12. Not yet audited.)

This one has no confirmed causal variant, and says so
-----------------------------------------------------

A simplification, not a claim about horse genetics
Most loci in this data name a real locus with a real published mutation behind it. CVM does not have one. It is a well-attested heritable malformation with a strong breed concentration and no agreed single gene, so the key here is the condition's own name (cvm) rather than a gene symbol invented to look respectable.

Treating it as one recessive locus is a simplification. It is written down here so that a later reader does not go looking for the paper.

The alternative was to leave it out. It earns its place because the simplification is honest at the level this data models anything - every locus here is one gene, one number, one outcome. Megaesophagus is the other gene in this position.

No affected horse in the population table
The estimated "general population" table lists only the clear horse and the carrier. An adult horse is one that survived, so it can carry this and never have it, and the homozygote's weight is simply absent from the table. The only way to produce an affected foal is to breed two carriers - which is what makes a pedigree worth keeping, and what makes carrier status the difference between a breeding programme and a lottery.
