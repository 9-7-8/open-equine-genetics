A stop codon in the spinal cord
-------------------------------

DMRT3 is expressed in a set of spinal-cord neurons that coordinate the left and right limbs. The gait variant is a nonsense mutation, Ser301STOP, found by Andersson et al. (2012) (https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11399) in Icelandic horses. It is permissive rather than simply dominant or recessive: it makes lateral gaits possible, and other genes and training decide which one a horse uses.

- Pace needs two copies. Almost every five-gaited Icelandic is A/A, but only about 70% of A/A Icelandics actually pace.
- One copy improves the tölt and still scores well at walk, trot, canter and gallop.
- Two copies give the best tölt and pace and a poorer trot and canter.
- In Standardbreds, trotters and pacers are both A/A, so other loci decide trot versus pace, and A/A horses race better than C/A ones.

The mutation arose within the last ten thousand years. The earliest ancient DNA carrying it is from England around 850-900 AD, and it reached Iceland soon after. Breeds selected for smooth riding gaits have fixed it; breeds selected for the gallop have not.

What the mod does not model yet: which lateral gait a gaited horse prefers, which is the modifier loci plus training, and the gait itself. Both wait for movement and animation work.
