ROUND FOUR (the owner's B4GALT7 overview, 2026-10-15)
-----------------------------------------------------
No address has been opened by a maintainer. Her answer, condensed, with every point and
address she gave. It agrees with round three and adds detail.

  - The variant: ECA14:g.4535550C>T (EquCab2.0), c.50G>A, p.Arg17Lys, the last base of
    exon 1. It disrupts the splice donor; an affected dwarf's fibroblasts had about 2% of
    normal B4GALT7 mRNA. Splicing, not the amino-acid change, is the likelier cause.
    (Leegwater et al. 2016, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5084406/)
  - N/N clear, N/D an unaffected carrier, D/D affected; UC Davis writes N and D and
    tests Friesians and crosses, also in its three-test Friesian Health Panel (no address
    given for the panel). (https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/friesian-horse-dwarfism)
  - D/D: short limbs and ribs, head and back length near normal; disorganised growth
    plates; flexor-tendon laxity and fetlock hyperextension, often worsening with age;
    rotation at carpus and hocks, abnormal gait; thickened, S-shaped costochondral
    junctions and an inward chest at thoracic vertebrae 10-16; broad chest, long back,
    rounded abdomen, poor musculature (probably secondary). The growth-hormone axis is
    not the primary cause; cognition is not impaired.
  - Frequencies: 22 of 177 (12.4%) is a DNA-bank validation set, not a survey, and
    holds no D/D horses; UC Davis's "about 12%" is a rounded figure; KFPS testing gave
    about 15% (2000) and 12.5% (2021), via Hermans et al. 2024; "roughly 12-15%". The
    Brazilian 33% of 12 horses is anecdotal and not a breed figure.
  - A 2026 genome-wide screen of 8,263 Friesians (BMC Genomics; no address given) lists
    the B4GALT7 splice variant as causal among several harmful haplotypes, whose carrier
    frequencies lie between 8.0% and 22.1% (not given for B4GALT7 alone).
  - No other equine B4GALT7 allele is established. No known effect in carriers (height,
    any skeletal sign), and none on coat, white, feathering, fertility or ordinary
    height. Severity varies among D/D horses; that is presentation, not further alleles.
  - The founder: the variant traces to one founder haplotype in the Dutch Friesian
    population; the study worked with KFPS.
  - Its modelling advice (not a finding): carrier x carrier matings flagged; D/D unfit
    for athletic use or breeding.

What the gene file does with it: the dwarfism text gains the joint, rib, gait and chest
signs and that thinking is unaffected; the summary gains the 2% mRNA, "about 12 to 15
per cent", that no other allele is established, and what B4GALT7 does not affect. Tables
unchanged.

ROUND THREE (the owner's answer to question G4, first part, 2026-10-15)

No address has been opened by a maintainer. Her answer, condensed, with every point and
address she gave:

  - "About 12%" comes from the discovery study (Leegwater et al. 2016): 22 carriers in
    177 Friesians genotyped, 12.4%; 155 clear. Eight known or obligate carriers used in
    the segregation analysis were all carriers, and the 29 dwarfs available were all
    homozygous. The paper does not describe the 177 as a random sample of the breed: it
    was the validation set after the mutation was found, so an early estimate, not a
    census. (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5084406/)
  - Current: about 12.5% carriers in 2021, from KFPS testing, cited by Hermans et al.
    2024; about 15% in 2000; the authors call it a decreasing trend over two decades.
    (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jbg.12872)
  - UC Davis's "about 12%" is the rounded 2016 figure.
    (https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/friesian-horse-dwarfism)
  - 33% among 12 actively breeding, unrelated Friesians in Brazil: a real sample figure,
    tiny and selected, not comparable.
    (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0737080625001492)
  - "About 1 in 7" (14.3%): an early breeder-facing estimate from FHANA/KFPS, in line
    with the older ~15%. (https://fhana.com/news/3024009-stallion-choices-for-mares-with-positive/)
  - KFPS has restricted carrier-to-carrier matings since 2014. The allele belongs to
    Friesians and their crosses; do not apply the figure to other breeds.
  - The MET part of G4 was not answered.

What B4GALT7 does
-----------------

B4GALT7 encodes beta-1,4-galactosyltransferase 7, a Golgi enzyme needed to build the carbohydrate “linker” that attaches glycosaminoglycan chains to proteoglycans. Those proteoglycans are important structural components of extracellular matrix, cartilage, tendon, ligament, and developing bone. [4][9]

The horse mutation associated with Friesian dwarfism is c.50G>A, producing p.Arg17Lys. It affects the final nucleotide of exon 1 and disrupts normal splicing of B4GALT7 transcripts, reducing normal gene function. [10][13]

Alleles and inheritance
-----------------------

Genotype | UC Davis notation | Phenotype | Breeding meaning
Normal homozygote | N/N | Unaffected | Cannot pass on this dwarfism variant. [11]
Carrier | D/N or N/D | Unaffected carrier | Can pass D to 50% of foals. [11]
Affected | D/D | Friesian dwarfism | Has two copies of the disease-associated variant. [11]

This is autosomal recessive, not dominant and not sex-linked. A cross of two carriers, D/N × D/N, produces a 25% chance of an affected D/D foal, a 50% chance of a carrier, and a 25% chance of N/N. [11]

Effect on the horse
-------------------

Affected Friesians have disproportionate dwarfism: limb and rib bones are shortened, while head size and back length remain comparatively normal. They also show hyperextension/laxity of the fetlock joints, with severity varying among individuals. [11]

The underlying defect interferes with normal extracellular-matrix and collagen-related development. Reported features include poor growth, short limbs, rib deformity, joint hypermobility, and poorly developed musculature. [11][12]

Does it affect coat color?
--------------------------

No established effect on coat color has been demonstrated.

- It does not control red versus black pigment; that is primarily the MC1R / ASIP system. [14]
- It does not act like cream, dun, silver, champagne, pearl, or gray dilution genes.
- It does not establish a mane/tail color, white-marking, feather-color, or hair-texture effect.
- The Friesian breed’s black coat and feathering are separate traits and should not be attributed to B4GALT7. [16]

A D/N carrier can be black, bay, chestnut, or carry any other color genotype; the dwarfism allele is independent of those loci in the evidence currently available.

Body systems: established vs. speculative
-----------------------------------------

Trait | Evidence for B4GALT7 D/D | Status

Bone structure | Shortened limbs and ribs; defective extracellular-matrix development | Established [11]
Joints / tendons / ligaments | Fetlock hyperextension and joint laxity | Established [11]
Muscle | Poorly developed musculature reported in affected horses | Established clinically, mechanism less precisely defined [12]
Hoof shape | No specific primary hoof-shape allele effect established | Not established
Feathering | No evidence that the allele causes or changes feathering | Not established [16]
Mane / tail color | No evidence of effect | Not established
Red / black pigment | No evidence of effect | Not established [14]
Speed / athletic performance | No direct evidence for an effect in N/N or D/N horses | Speculative; do not model as established
Subtle size reduction in carriers | Sometimes suggested informally, but UC Davis states carriers are unaffected | Not established [11]

Breeds affected
---------------

The known disease-associated variant is primarily a Friesian issue. UC Davis lists Friesians and Friesian crosses as the appropriate testing population. [11]

At the time of UC Davis’s summary, approximately 12% of Friesians were estimated to be carriers (D/N). That figure can change over time as breeders use testing and avoid carrier-to-carrier matings. [11]

Testing and breeder use
-----------------------

UC Davis offers a direct DNA test for the B4GALT7 Friesian dwarfism variant. Its purpose is to identify carriers before breeding, especially to prevent D/N × D/N pairings that can produce affected D/D foals. [11]

Controversy and open questions
------------------------------

The core association between the B4GALT7 variant and Friesian dwarfism is well supported, but several practical questions remain open:

- Whether D/N carriers have any measurable reduction in stature, bone quality, joint resilience, or athletic durability.
- Whether other B4GALT7 variants exist in horses or whether the same phenotype can arise from other genes in the proteoglycan-linker pathway.
- How variable D/D severity is, and how much management, nutrition, hoof care, and veterinary support modify the visible phenotype.
- Whether the allele occurs outside Friesians or Friesian crosses at meaningful frequency.

Human medicine shows that pathogenic B4GALT7 variants can cause spondylodysplastic Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, with short stature, hypotonia, joint hypermobility, skeletal anomalies, and connective-tissue signs. That supports the biological plausibility of a skeletal/connective-tissue effect in horses, but human variants and phenotypes should not be treated as identical to the equine Friesian variant. [1][3][8]

Bottom line
-----------

Treat B4GALT7 as a health-and-conformation locus, not a color gene. N is normal, D is the Friesian dwarfism allele, D/N horses are healthy carriers, and D/D horses have disproportionate dwarfism with joint laxity. There is no good evidence that it changes red/black pigment, mane or tail color, feathering, hoof shape, or speed. [11][14][16]
