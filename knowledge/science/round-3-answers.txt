# B3 — New Coat-Colour Allele Frequencies by Breed

**Primary frequency source:** Avila et al. (2022), *Genes* 13(9):1641 — a UC Davis Veterinary Genetics Laboratory study of 29 variants in 11,281 horses from 28 breeds. Frequencies below are **relatedness-filtered allele frequencies (a.f.)** unless noted. [page:4][web:1]

**Simulation note:** Store these as breed-specific allele-frequency priors, not universal probabilities. Most dilution alleles are highly breed-structured; a single global carrier rate would badly distort foal generation.

## Dun: `D`, `nd1`, `nd2`

Dun is the ancestral, dominant dilution at *TBX3*. `D` dilutes the body while leaving points relatively dark and commonly adds a dorsal stripe and other primitive markings; `nd1` retains possible primitive markings without body dilution; `nd2` is the ordinary non-dun allele. [web:3][page:4]

| Breed | `D` a.f. | `nd1` a.f. | `nd2` a.f. |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Norwegian Fjord | 1.00 | 0 | 0 [page:4] |
| Pura Raza Española | Not listed as highest/lowest here | 0.79 | 0.20 [page:4] |
| Gypsy Vanner | 0 | 0 | 1.00 [page:4] |
| Tennessee Walking Horse | 0.0060 | Present | Present [page:4] |
| Gypsy Cob | Present | 0.017 | Present [page:4] |

`D` was not detected in Arabian, Dutch Warmblood, Gypsy Vanner, Hanoverian, Knabstrupper, Oldenburg, Percheron, Standardbred, or Thoroughbred in this dataset. [page:4]

**Simulator implementation:** Treat dun as a three-allele ordered system: `D > nd1 > nd2`. Phenotype requires only the presence of `D`; `nd1/nd1` or `nd1/nd2` can add primitive markings as a separate visual-expression layer with breed- and individual-level variability.

## Extension: `e` and `ea`

`E` permits black pigment production. Recessive `e` prevents functional eumelanin production, producing chestnut regardless of agouti genotype; `ea` is a second recessive chestnut allele at *MC1R*. [page:4]

- `e` was found in all 28 breeds, averaging 0.44; it ranged from 0.76 in Pony of the Americas to 0.12 in Percheron. [page:4]
- `ea` was detected in Knabstrupper, Paint Horse, Percheron, and Quarter Horse. [page:4]
- Knabstrupper had an estimated `ea` frequency of 0.035 after relatedness filtering. [page:4]
- Earlier reports placed `ea` in Black Forest Horse, Hungarian Coldblood, and Haflinger; the UC Davis study expanded its known breed distribution. [page:4]

**Simulator implementation:** Model `E`, `e`, and `ea` as recessive loss-of-function alternatives to `E`. For phenotype, `e/e`, `ea/ea`, and `e/ea` are all chestnut; for breeding, preserve the allele identity because `e/ea` is compound heterozygous but still chestnut.

## Agouti: `a`

The recessive `a` allele at *ASIP* removes restriction of black pigment to the points, allowing an `E/_` horse to be black. [page:4]

|---|---:|
| Percheron | 0.99 [page:4] |
| Gypsy Vanner | 0.92 [page:4] |
| Rocky Mountain Horse | 0.87 [page:4] |
| Tennessee Walking Horse | 0.86 [page:4] |
| Gypsy Cob | 0.82 [page:4] |
| Thoroughbred | 0.24 [page:4] |
| Norwegian Fjord | 0.24 [page:4] |

The cross-breed mean was 0.58, and `a` occurred in every breed studied. [page:4]

**Simulator implementation:** Use a simple two-allele system: `A` restricts black to points in an `E/_` horse; `a/a` produces black. Agouti is masked by any chestnut genotype.

## Cream, pearl, and sunshine

Cream, pearl, and sunshine are all alleles at or associated with *SLC45A2* (historically called *MATP*), but they are distinct variants with different visual effects and inheritance interactions. [page:4][web:15][web:22]

| Allele | Breed | a.f. |
|---|---|---:|
| `Cr` | Lusitano | 0.42 [page:4] |
| `Cr` | Standardbred | 0.013 [page:4] |
| `Cr` | 25 of 28 breeds | Present; absent in Hanoverian, Knabstrupper, Percheron [page:4] |
| `Prl` | Andalusian | 0.12 [page:4] |
| `Prl` | Pura Raza Española | 0.11 [page:4] |
| `Prl` | Lusitano | 0.064 [page:4] |
| `Prl` | Gypsy Cob | 0.017 [page:4] |
| `Prl` | Gypsy Vanner | 0.015 [page:4] |
| `Prl` | Paint Horse | 0.0070 [page:4] |
| `Prl` | Quarter Horse | 0.00031 [page:4] |
| `SUN` | Standardbred / Tennessee Walking Horse-related lineage | No population estimate; very rare [web:18][web:22] |

`Cr/Cr` produces a double-dilute phenotype on any base; `Cr/Prl` and `Cr/SUN` can produce pseudo-double-dilute appearances despite only one cream copy. `Prl/Prl` produces pearl dilution, while single-copy pearl carriers usually look undiluted. [web:15][web:22]

**Simulator implementation:** Keep `Cr`, `Prl`, and `SUN` as separate alleles at one dilution-complex locus, with a phenotype matrix rather than simple dominance:

- `N/N`: no dilution.
- `Cr/N`: palomino, buckskin, or smoky black depending on base.
- `Cr/Cr`: double cream dilute.
- `Prl/Prl`: pearl.
- `Cr/Prl` and `Cr/SUN`: pseudo-double dilute.
- `SUN/SUN`: expected sunshine dilution, but phenotype data remain too sparse for confident visual rules.

## Silver

Silver `Z` dilutes eumelanin, so it visibly affects black-based coats and the black mane/tail of bays; it has little obvious effect on chestnut. [page:4]

| Breed | `Z` a.f. |
|---|---:|
| Rocky Mountain Horse | 0.32 [page:4] |
| Quarter Horse | 0.0011 [page:4] |
| 15 breeds total | Detected [page:4] |

Silver is medically relevant: `Z/Z` horses can have more severe multiple congenital ocular anomalies (MCOA), while `Z/N` horses more often have cysts only. [page:4]

**Simulator implementation:** Model `Z` as dominant for coat phenotype, but add a health/ocular-risk flag for `Z/Z`, especially in Rocky Mountain Horses, Miniature Horses, Icelandic Horses, Shetlands, Morgans, and other breeds where it occurs.

## Mushroom

Mushroom `Mu` is recessive and was tested only in Icelandic Horse, Miniature Horse, Quarter Horse, and Shetland Pony in the UC Davis dataset; it was detected only in Shetland Pony, at a relatedness-filtered frequency of 0.23. [page:4]

- `Mu/Mu` reduces pheomelanin, creating a dilute sepia coat in chestnut horses. [page:4]
- In bay horses, it can lighten red body pigment while producing darker countershading, suggesting a more complex effect on pigment balance than a simple “red diluter.” [web:10]
- The allele is strongly associated with Shetland-type ponies; its presence in Miniature Horses remains plausible but was not detected in that UC Davis sample. [page:4]

**Simulator implementation:** Restrict `Mu` generation heavily to Shetland and Shetland-influenced pony populations. Use recessive expression, but implement bay mushroom as a distinct coat recipe rather than merely applying the chestnut mushroom color.

## Tiger eye: `TE1` and `TE2`

Tiger eye is an iris-color trait, not a coat dilution. It is inherited recessively and produces yellow, amber, or orange irises; UC Davis identifies both variants in *SLC24A5*. [web:13][web:11]

| Genotype | Expected eye phenotype |
|---|---|
| `N/N` | Normal brown eye [web:13] |
| `N/TE1` or `N/TE2` | Brown-eyed carrier [web:13] |
| `TE1/TE1` | Tiger eye; most common affected genotype [web:13][web:11] |
| `TE1/TE2` | Tiger eye [web:13][web:11] |
| `TE2/TE2` | Tiger eye; extremely rare, with one documented case [web:13] |

In a Puerto Rican Paso Fino study of 154 horses, 32 had tiger eye: 20 were `TE1/TE1` and 8 were `TE1/TE2`. The remaining tiger-eye cases were not explained by these two variants, implying at least one additional genetic cause. [web:11][page:14]

Testing of 196 horses from Colombian Paso, Mangalarga, Lusitano, and Andalusian populations found no tiger-eye alleles, supporting strong breed specificity. [web:11]

**Simulator implementation:** Make `TE1` and `TE2` recessive eye-color alleles at *SLC24A5*. Any combination of two tiger-eye alleles expresses tiger eye. Add an unexplained-tiger-eye fallback trait for the roughly 12% of affected study horses not explained by `TE1` or `TE2`. [web:11]

## What these genes do **not** establish

None of the alleles listed here has robust evidence for controlling height, speed, muscle mass, bone structure, hoof shape, feathering, mane or tail length, or general body conformation. Their established primary effects are pigment production, pigment distribution, hair-coat color, skin pigmentation, iris color, and—in silver—ocular development. [page:4][web:13]

The main non-cosmetic implementation flags are:

- **Silver `Z/Z`:** elevated MCOA/ocular risk. [page:4]
- **Cream, pearl, champagne, and tiger eye:** eye and skin appearance may change, but they should not be treated as performance genes. [web:13][page:4]
- **Dun `nd1`:** primitive markings are a coat-expression trait, not a body-structure trait. [web:3]

## Controversy and future work

- **Sunshine:** Its breed distribution and population frequency are unresolved; it appears extremely rare and is known chiefly from a limited Standardbred/Tennessee Walking Horse-related lineage. [web:18][web:22]
- **Mushroom outside Shetlands:** The UC Davis panel tested only four breeds for `Mu`; absence in Miniature Horses in that sample does not prove absence breed-wide. [page:4]
- **Tiger eye:** `TE1` and `TE2` explain most, but not all, Puerto Rican Paso Fino tiger-eye cases, so additional variants likely remain undiscovered. [web:11]
- **`ea`:** Its apparent rarity partly reflects limited historical testing; broader genomic screening will likely find it in additional breeds. [page:4]
- **Breed-frequency bias:** UC Davis frequencies derive from submitted test samples, not necessarily random breed censuses; use them as priors and update them as more representative genomic datasets become available. [page:4]

# C6 — White-pattern allele frequencies by breed

The best current population-scale reference is Avila et al. (2022), a UC Davis VGL study of **11,281 horses from 28 breeds**, with relatedness-filtered allele frequencies for **SB1, W5, W10, W20, W22, SW1–SW6**.  For **W13, W32, W34, W35, SW7, and SW8**, no comparable multi-breed frequency table yet exists; they are best modeled as rare, lineage- or breed-associated variants. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9498372/)

## Frequency conventions

- **a.f.** = allele frequency: proportion of all alleles in the sampled breed that are the variant.
- **Carrier share** ≈ 2 × a.f. when the allele is rare and Hardy–Weinberg assumptions are reasonable.
- UC Davis filtered first-degree relatives before estimating frequencies, which is preferable for simulator base rates because it reduces founder-horse inflation. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9498372/)
- These are **testing-cohort frequencies**, not necessarily breed-wide census frequencies; testing is often biased toward patterned horses or breeding stock. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9498372/)

## Epistasis and health effects

- White percentage rises with the number of spotting alleles: in 1,195 Paint Horses, median white coat rose from 4% with zero tested spotting alleles to 19% with two, 53% with three, and 100% in the single five-allele horse. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10671537/)
- W20 alone is real but mild: W20/N Paints averaged a median 5% white versus 4% for N/N, while SB1/N and SW1/N were around 10%. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10671537/)
- Strong combinations matter: TO/N + W20/N had a median 48% white; O/N + SB1/N reached 95.5%; and TO/N + O/N + W20/N reached 78.5%. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10671537/)
- Chestnut base color increased white expression in Paints, indicating MC1R–white-spotting epistasis; model this as a multiplier or additive bonus rather than making base color directly cause white. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10671537/)
- Deafness is associated with some splashed-white phenotypes, particularly extreme MITF/PAX3-related white; SW8 was identified in a deaf, nearly all-white Thoroughbred offspring. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/splashed-white)
- Presumed homozygous lethals in this set include **W5, W10, SW3**, and likely **SW5/SW6**; UC Davis specifically recommends avoiding SW3-to-SW3 matings. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9498372/)

## Recommended simulator model

Use a **two-layer system**:

1. **Inheritance layer:** each allele is an independent autosomal dominant allele with its own breed-specific prior; W20 is additive rather than strongly visual alone.
2. **Phenotype layer:** calculate a continuous `whiteScore`, then map it to pattern class using allele-specific distribution rules.

A useful formula is:

whiteScore = sum_i w_i g_i + sum_{i<j} e_ij g_i g_j + b_chestnut + epsilon

where g_i is 0, 1, or 2 copies of allele i, w_i is its main effect, e_ij captures combinations such as TO+W20, O+SB1, and SW1+SW2, b_chestnut is the chestnut epistatic bonus, and epsilon represents developmental randomness. The Paint study found that even a neural-net model explained only about 55.5% of white-coat variation in validation, so retain meaningful stochasticity. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10671537/)

For visual realism, separate **amount of white** from **pattern geography**: SB1 favors jagged high-leg/ventral white, SW favors broad blazes and belly white, TO creates crossing white patterns, and W alleles range from subtle sabino-like markings to near-complete depigmentation. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10854722/)
