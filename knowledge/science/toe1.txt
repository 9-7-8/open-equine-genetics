- Cerebellar abiotrophy (CA) in Arabians is an inherited, untreatable loss of the
  Purkinje cells of the cerebellum after birth. https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/ca
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0888754310002430
- The associated variant is TOE1 g.2171G>A on chromosome 2 (ECA2:13,074,277G>A in
  earlier coordinates); A is the CA allele. It is in exon 4 of TOE1 and changes
  arginine to histidine at position 95 (R95H). It also lies in the promoter region of
  MUTYH, which is read from the opposite strand; affected horses show altered local DNA
  methylation and altered MUTYH expression. Whether the disease comes through MUTYH,
  TOE1 or both is still being worked out.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5905334/
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3671216/
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5336519/
- Autosomal recessive. UC Davis writes N (normal) and CA: N/CA is a clinically normal
  carrier, CA/CA is affected.
- Signs usually begin at about six weeks, from birth to as late as four months: an
  intention head tremor, ataxia with a wide stance, exaggerated high action of the
  forelegs, a poor menace response, easy startling, falls and difficulty rising. The
  horse keeps its muscle strength. Severity runs from a mild head tremor to disabling.
  No cure; severely affected foals are often put down.
  https://ker.com/equinews/arabian-horse-mutation-linked-cerebellar-abiotrophy/
- Almost exclusively in Arabians; the test also suits breeds with Arabian ancestry,
  among them the Danish Sport Horse, Trakehner and Welsh Pony.
- UC Davis reported an average carrier frequency of about 20% across the Arabian breed,
  from screening nearly 11,000 horses; it differs widely between families.

TOE1 is not an established horse coat-color locus. In horses, the gene that directly controls red versus black base pigment is Extension (MC1R)—often called the red factor—so “toe1” may be a typo or shorthand for that locus. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/resources/horse-coat-color)

What TOE1 actually is
---------------------

TOE1 (Target of EGR1, exonuclease) encodes a nuclear RNA-processing protein with poly(A)-specific ribonuclease activity. It participates in small nuclear RNA 3′-end processing and has been implicated in cell-cycle regulation and telomerase RNA processing. [genecards](https://www.genecards.org/card/TOE1)

In model organisms, loss or knockdown of Toe1 can disrupt early embryonic development; in mice, knockdown embryos arrest around the morula-to-blastocyst transition.  This makes TOE1 a basic cellular/RNA-processing gene, not a known pigment-switching gene. [sciencedirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0093691X22004198)

Is there a horse TOE1 coat-color allele?
----------------------------------------

No recognized equine coat-color database or UC Davis testing resource identifies a TOE1 locus, allele, or pigment phenotype in horses. UC Davis’s equine coat-color overview instead identifies MC1R and ASIP as the core genes for the red/black base-color system. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/resources/horse-coat-color)

Topic | Established finding
TOE1 in horses | No established coat-color allele or validated phenotype.  [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/resources/horse-coat-color)
Red vs. black pigment | Controlled primarily by MC1R / Extension.  [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/resources/horse-coat-color)
Distribution of black pigment | Controlled by ASIP / Agouti.  [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/resources/horse-coat-color)
Known MC1R alleles | E, e, and rare e^a.  [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/resources/horse-coat-color)

If you meant Extension (MC1R)
-----------------------------

The Extension locus determines whether a horse can make eumelanin, the black pigment. At least one functional E allele permits black pigment; e/e horses cannot produce it and are red-based. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/resources/horse-coat-color)

Genotype | Pigment effect | Typical base phenotype
E/E | Black pigment produced | Black-based; cannot produce a red-based foal
E/e | Black pigment produced; carries red | Black-based; can produce red-based foals
e/e | Black pigment not produced | Chestnut/sorrel or another red-based color
E/e^a or e/e^a | e^a is a rare recessive loss-of-function allele | Red-based only if no functional E is present

UC Davis lists E as the wild-type allele, with recessive e and rare e^a producing red pigment; e/e and e/e^a horses produce only pheomelanin, or red pigment. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/resources/horse-coat-color)

Effects on other traits
-----------------------

There is no credible evidence that TOE1 affects horse height, speed, muscle mass, bone structure, hoof shape, feathering, mane or tail color, or any coat pattern. Those traits are governed by different genes or polygenic architecture; for example, gray is associated with a STX17 duplication, while white patterning and dilution traits involve separate loci. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11200706/)

Likewise, MC1R affects pigment type, not conformation or athletic traits. A chestnut can be tall or short, fast or slow, heavily feathered or clean-legged; MC1R only determines that the visible base pigment is red rather than black. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/resources/horse-coat-color)

Breeds
------

Because TOE1 has no established horse coat-color role, it has no breed distribution relevant to color breeding. MC1R, by contrast, occurs across essentially all horse breeds; its frequency differs by breed because selection and founder effects shape how often red-based horses appear. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/resources/horse-coat-color)

Controversy and future science
------------------------------

The main uncertainty is nomenclature rather than pigment genetics: “TOE1” is a real gene name, but it is not a recognized equine color locus. Future equine genomics may identify additional rare MC1R variants or modifier genes, but any claim that TOE1 creates a horse color, pattern, or performance trait would need direct horse-specific evidence. [vgl.ucdavis](https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/resources/horse-coat-color)
