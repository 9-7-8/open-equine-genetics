- Strong family and breed evidence in Friesians (necropsy of 852 horses; about 2% of Friesians at two Dutch clinics against 0.5%; 41 of 45 Dutch cases Friesian; abnormal collagen in young foals); candidate variants in 2021, no mutation or test. Not described as fatal at birth: the severity went from "lethal_at_birth" to "impairing" and the outcome text was rewritten (session's choice).

No confirmed causal variant
---------------------------

Like CVM, this is a breed condition that is clearly heritable and has no agreed single gene behind it, so it is keyed on the condition rather than on a gene symbol and the simplification to one recessive locus is made in this data. See that note for the reasoning, which is the same reasoning.

No affected horse in the population table
The estimated "general population" table lists only the clear horse and the carrier. An adult horse is one that survived, so it can carry this and never have it, and the homozygote's weight is simply absent from the table. The only way to produce an affected foal is to breed two carriers - which is what makes a pedigree worth keeping, and what makes carrier status the difference between a breeding programme and a lottery.
