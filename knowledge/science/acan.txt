# ACAN D3* and D4: are Generatio's variants the published ones?

**Short answer:** Generatio's **D3\*** is the same corrected variant published by Eberth et al. (2018) as **D3\***: `EquCab3.0 chr1:g.95282140C>G`. Generatio's **D4** is biologically the same published D4 allele, but Generatio's displayed genomic interval, `g.95257458_95257500del`, is **43 bp long** and does not match the primary paper's 21-bp D4 coordinate, `g.95257480_95257500del`. The primary-paper coordinate should be treated as authoritative unless Generatio documents a different reference/transcript convention.

## Assembly and coordinates

All four established pathogenic ACAN alleles were reported relative to **EquCab3.0 / GCF_002863925.1**, on horse chromosome 1 (`NC_009144.3`). [web:1][web:2]

| Generatio label | Generatio coordinate | Published primary-paper coordinate | Same variant? |
|---|---|---|---|
| D3* | `g.95282140C>G` | `g.95282140C>G` (`rs1095048823`) | **Yes** |
| D4 | `g.95257458_95257500del` | `g.95257480_95257500del` (`rs1095048839`) | **Same allele, but Generatio's start coordinate appears inconsistent** |

The primary paper explicitly designates the four variants, in order, as D1, D2, D3*, and D4, with D3* at `g.95282140C>G` and D4 at `g.95257480_95257500del`. [web:1][web:16]

## D3 versus D3*

The asterisk in **D3\*** is a deliberate label meaning “corrected D3.” The original D3 report described a single-base deletion in exon 11, `c.6700delC` / `p.P1875fx`. Later work found that this result was a **technical sequencing artifact**, not the true causative variant. [web:2][web:4][web:12]

The real pathogenic allele is a missense substitution, `g.95282140C>G`, causing `p.Ala505Pro` in the aggrecan interglobular domain. Eberth et al. renamed it **D3\*** to distinguish it from the erroneous original D3. [web:1][web:2][web:4]

Therefore:

- **Original D3:** `c.6700delC`, one-base deletion — **not a valid disease allele; artifact.**
- **D3\*:** `EquCab3.0:g.95282140C>G`, missense — **the variant tested and reported by Generatio.**
- They are **not the same variant**; D3\* replaced the discredited original D3.

## D4 coordinate discrepancy

The published D4 allele is a **21-bp deletion** affecting exon 14/15 depending on transcript annotation, with protein effect `p.Phe2017-Asp2023del` in one annotation and `p.Phe2433-Gln2440del`/similar in another. OMIA records the genomic change as `NC_009144.3:g.95257480_95257500del` on EquCab3.0. [web:2][web:4]

Generatio writes D4 as:

```text
g.95257458_95257500del
```

That interval contains:

```text
95257500 - 95257458 + 1 = 43 bases
```

So it cannot literally describe the same 21-bp deletion if both endpoints use the same coordinate system. The likely explanations are:

1. **Generatio's displayed start coordinate is erroneous or uses an unstated convention.**
2. Generatio may be showing a larger reference interval around the deletion rather than the precise deleted bases, but its own “43 bases” wording makes that unlikely as a precise HGVS-style deletion.
3. The biologically tested allele is still D4, because Generatio explicitly identifies it as D4 and uses the same variant name. [web:8]

**For a simulation or database, record D4 as the 21-bp deletion at `EquCab3.0:g.95257480_95257500del`, and flag Generatio's `g.95257458_95257500del` as a coordinate-display discrepancy—not as a fifth independent allele.**

## cDNA naming differences

The cDNA descriptions differ because they are relative to different ACAN transcripts:

- `c.7299_7319del` is the 21-bp deletion in one transcript annotation. [web:2]
- `c.7633_7653del` is the same genomic deletion described against another transcript. [web:3]
- This does **not** imply two different D4 mutations; genomic coordinates and the 21-bp deletion length are the stable identifiers.

## Simulator implementation

Model ACAN as a single autosomal recessive disease locus with four functionally equivalent pathogenic alleles:

```text
N  = normal
d1 = ACAN D1
d2 = ACAN D2
d3 = ACAN D3*
d4 = ACAN D4
```

Phenotype rule:

- `N/N` — normal
- `N/d1`, `N/d2`, `N/d3`, `N/d4` — clinically normal carrier
- Any two pathogenic alleles — chondrodysplastic dwarfism, including compound heterozygotes such as `d2/d4` or `d3/d4` [web:1][web:4][web:8]

Do **not** model original D3 as a separate allele. Either omit it or retain it only as a historical/lab-report alias mapped to “invalid/artifact.”

Why this is not "is it homozygous?"
-----------------------------------

A D1/D4 horse is affected. So is D2/D3. The five variants are different broken versions of the same gene, and what matters is that the horse has no working copy, not that its two copies match.

Every other disorder in this data can be written as "count the variant allele and check for two". This one cannot, and the check has to be a predicate over the whole pair. It is the clearest argument in the model for a combination table over a dominance label: recessive describes nine of these ten affected combinations and quietly loses the compound heterozygotes.

What that means at the paddock gate
Two carriers of different variants are exactly as dangerous as two carriers of the same one. "My two lines carry different mutations" is not the safety it sounds like.
