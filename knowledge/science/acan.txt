ROUND FOUR (the owner's ACAN overview, answering G6 and part of G7, 2026-10-15)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
No address has been opened by a maintainer. Her answer, condensed, with every point and
address she gave. Where it disagrees with the rounds below, this section and the gene
file stand.

  - Established alleles: N, D1 c.245del (p.K82fs), D2 c.1270G>A (p.V424M), D3*
    c.6700delC (p.P1875fs) and D4 c.7299_7319del (Eberth et al. 2018, via OMIA,
    https://omia.org/OMIA001271/9796/). This confirms the c. names in the gene file. The
    asterisk on D3* is from the original naming and marks the established allele; many
    breeder pages write D3. (The protein change printed for D4, "p.F2433_O2440del", has
    a letter that is no amino acid and is not in the data.)
  - D5 (g.95258999C>T) is proposed, not established: a University of Kentucky
    dissertation reports that it segregates apart from D1-D4 and the Brazilian
    haplotype; CombiBreed offers a test (https://www.combibreed.com/acand5/). Generatio
    lists only D1-D4; UC Davis has no ACAN page. Keep D5 out of the core model.
  - A Brazilian missense variant, c.6465A>T (p.Leu2155Phe), is "potentially associated"
    with dwarfism (Scientific Reports 2020). It is not D5 and must not be renamed D5.
    Dwarfs carrying only one of D2, D3* or D4, or none, imply further variants.
  - Pairs: as in round three. D1 with D1, D2 or D3*: prenatal loss. D1/D4: usually
    prenatal loss, a rare live severe dwarf
    (https://www.laboklin.co.uk/laboklin/showGeneticTest.jsp?testID=8548). Pairs of D2,
    D3* and D4 are live dwarfs, D2 pairs comparatively milder, D3*/D3*, D3*/D4 and
    D4/D4 moderate to severe. D3*/D3* is expected live but little documented; D4/D4 is
    documented in two Miniature Horses.
  - Frequencies in Miniature Horses, by allele (Eberth et al., via OMIA): D1 0.03, D2
    0.09, D3* 0.02, D4 0.03, summing to about 0.17. The 0.280 is the four together in a
    Brazilian study (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7499210/), implying about
    40.3% carriers and 7.8% affected under random mating; it is not to be split by
    allele.
  - Height: in 245 Miniature Horses, carriers of any of D1-D4 averaged 1.43 inches
    shorter, with much overlap (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32065671/; the answer
    names the authors Graves, Eberth and Bailey).
  - No direct effect on pigment, mane or tail colour, feathering, white markings, hoof
    colour, speed or muscle. Effects are skeletal; any loss of performance comes through
    conformation and soundness.
  - Its modelling advice (not a finding): D1/D4 lost before birth 90-98% of the time; the
    answer says these percentages are not measured. Not in the data: the format has no
    chance of survival for a pair.

What the gene file does with it: D3's name is "D3*" (the id stays D3). The summary gives
the per-allele frequencies, the 0.280, and D5's dissertation and laboratory. A Miniature
Horse table is worked out from the four allele frequencies, assuming random mating, with
the three nonviable pairs left out; the american_miniature breed pool is the same table.
D5 is dropped from the "general population" table and the chincoteague_pony pool (which
held only D5) is removed. Three citations added.

The session's reading (not the answer's):
  - Round three put the 0.280 with Eberth et al. and the 98 carriers in 245 with
    "Andersson et al. 2020"; this round puts the 0.280 with the Brazilian study and the
    245 horses with Graves, Eberth and Bailey. Question G6.
  - The worked-out table counts D1/D4 at its random-mating share although most such foals
    are lost, and the sample's allele frequencies are of living horses.

ROUND THREE, SECOND ANSWER (the owner's answer to question G6, survival, 2026-10-15)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
No address has been opened by a maintainer. Her answer, condensed, with every point and
address she gave (from https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-72192-3 unless
marked):

  - D1/D1 foals do not normally live to birth: D1 is treated as an embryonic or fetal
    lethal when homozygous or with any other ACAN variant. The conceptus is reabsorbed
    or aborted, usually early in gestation; there is no foal lifespan to report.
  - D1/D1, D1/D2, D1/D3*: fetal death. D1/D4: usually fetal death, but at least one
    full-term live D1/D4 dwarf foal is documented, so rarely viable.
  - D2/D2, D2/D3*, D2/D4, D3*/D3*, D3*/D4 and D4/D4 can be born alive and may reach
    adulthood, as disproportionate dwarfs. Survival varies with severity, conformation,
    breathing and orthopaedic problems, pain and management; many are put down for
    poor quality of life rather than because the genotype cannot reach adulthood.
    (https://laboklin.com/en/products/genetics/hereditary-diseases/horse/dwarfism-acan-chondrodysplasia/)
  - D2/D2: generally among the less severe. D2/D4: live foal documented; D2 pairs often
    considered milder. D3*/D3*: very rare, adult data limited. D4/D4: live newborns
    documented, adult phenotype not well established
    (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7003212/).
  - Carriers (one variant, one N) look normal or near normal and are not dwarfs.

The answer writes D3 as "D3*", Generatio's name; whether that is the D3 of the gene file
is question G7.

What the gene file does with it: D1/D1, D1/D2 and D1/D3 become nonviable (absent from
the living genotypes). This changes the owner's "lethal_in_early_life" of 2026-10-12 for
D1/D1, which rested on round two's "survival time varies". D1/D4 stays a living pair
with an outcome of its own: usually lost before birth, a severe dwarf on the rare
occasion it is born. The dwarf outcome gains what is said of survival.

Why this is not "is it homozygous?"
-----------------------------------

A D1/D4 horse is affected. So is D2/D3. The five variants are different broken versions of the same gene, and what matters is that the horse has no working copy, not that its two copies match.

Every other disorder in this data can be written as "count the variant allele and check for two". This one cannot, and the check has to be a predicate over the whole pair. It is the clearest argument in the model for a combination table over a dominance label: recessive describes nine of these ten affected combinations and quietly loses the compound heterozygotes.

What that means at the paddock gate
Two carriers of different variants are exactly as dangerous as two carriers of the same one. "My two lines carry different mutations" is not the safety it sounds like.
