ROUND THREE (the owner's answer to question A2, 2026-10-15)
------------------------------------------------------------
No address has been opened by a maintainer. Her answer, condensed, with every point and
address she gave:

  - The SCID allele is a 5 bp deletion of TCTCA: NM_001163858.1:c.9478_9482del,
    also annotated NP_001157330.1:p.(N3160fs*3), a frameshift and premature stop that
    truncates the protein by about 967 C-terminal amino acids.
    (https://omia.org/OMIA000220/9796/)
  - On EquCab3.0 it is NC_009152.3:g.36395752_36395756del: positions 36,395,752 to
    36,395,756 on chromosome 9 are deleted. (OMIA)
  - Deleted sequence TCTCA (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8701198/). Gene
    PRKDC (DNA-PKcs), ECA9 (https://vgl.ucdavis.edu/test/scid).
  - Older papers and databases write c.4675_4679del: another transcript's numbering, not
    another mutation. OMIA and diagnostic laboratories now use c.9478_9482del.
  - UC Davis describes it as a five base-pair deletion in the DNA-dependent protein
    kinase gene that truncates the protein, and tests mainly Arabian and part-Arab horses.
  - The frameshift begins at codon 3160 with a stop three codons later; the protein lacks
    its C-terminal phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase domain, which impairs V(D)J
    recombination and so T- and B-cell development.
    (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7184946/)
  - Autosomal recessive: carriers are clinically normal, homozygotes have SCID.

What the gene file does with it: the summary gains the c., p. and EquCab3.0 names.

PRKDC (protein kinase, DNA-activated, catalytic polypeptide; formerly DNA-PKcs) is best known in horses as the gene behind Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID), a fatal autosomal-recessive immune defect concentrated in Arabian and part-Arabian horses. It is not a coat-color locus: it does not produce red, black, dilution, white-pattern, mane/tail-color, feathering, or conformational effects. [cite:23][cite:14]

Gene and locus
--------------

- Gene symbol: PRKDC; older equine literature calls it DNA-PK or DNA-PKcs. [cite:14]
- Protein: DNA-dependent protein kinase catalytic subunit (DNA-PKcs), a very large serine/threonine kinase. [cite:2][cite:5]
- Horse chromosome: ECA9, reported at 9p12 by classical mapping; OMIA lists the equine gene on chromosome 9. [cite:14][cite:12]
- Core function: DNA-PKcs joins the Ku70/Ku80 heterodimer to form DNA-dependent protein kinase (DNA-PK), which repairs DNA double-strand breaks through non-homologous end joining (NHEJ). [cite:2][cite:4]
- Immune role: The same NHEJ machinery is required for V(D)J recombination, the DNA rearrangement process that creates functional T-cell receptors and immunoglobulins. Without it, horses cannot develop a normal adaptive immune system. [cite:6][cite:7]

Established equine allele
-------------------------

There is one well-established pathogenic equine PRKDC variant, usually reported as a five-base-pair deletion in the gene.

UC Davis genotype | Meaning | Expected outcome
N/N | No SCID variant detected | Not affected by this SCID variant and cannot transmit it. [cite:23]
N/SCID | Heterozygous carrier | Clinically unaffected carrier; transmits the variant to about 50% of foals. [cite:23]
SCID/SCID | Homozygous for the deletion | Fatal severe combined immunodeficiency. [cite:23]

The deletion causes a frameshift and premature truncation of DNA-PKcs, eliminating kinase activity. That loss prevents normal coding- and signal-joint formation during V(D)J recombination, so affected foals lack functional B- and T-cell immunity. [cite:14][cite:16]

Inheritance and breeding impact
-------------------------------

SCID is autosomal recessive: a foal must inherit the mutant allele from both parents to be affected. Carrier-to-carrier matings produce a 25% affected, 50% carrier, and 25% clear expectation per foal. [cite:23]

This makes the allele especially important in Arabian breeding programs and in crosses involving Arabian ancestry. UC Davis notes that the condition occurs in Arabian and part-Arab horses; other summaries estimate roughly 0.2% affected prevalence in Arabians or Arabian crosses, while carrier frequency estimates in older literature vary substantially by population and sampling period. [cite:23][cite:16][cite:24]

Clinical effects
----------------

Affected foals are not simply “weak” foals: they have a profound failure of adaptive immunity.

- Recurrent or overwhelming bacterial, viral, fungal, and protozoal infections. [cite:23]
- Common signs include pneumonia, diarrhea, lethargy, poor growth, and failure to respond normally to infection. [cite:16]
- The disease is ultimately fatal; affected foals generally do not survive beyond roughly four to six months, often dying from opportunistic infection. [cite:16]
- Carriers are clinically normal for SCID, though one veterinary genetics summary raises a possible association between carrier status and higher incidence of virus-associated sarcoids; this should be treated as a secondary, less firmly established observation rather than a core PRKDC phenotype. [cite:16]

Effects on pigment and conformation
-----------------------------------

PRKDC has no established role in any visible coat, pigment, or structural trait in horses.

Trait | Established PRKDC effect? | Notes
Red pigment / chestnut | No | Red versus black pigment is governed chiefly by MC1R and ASIP, not PRKDC.
Black pigment | No | PRKDC is not part of the extension/agouti system.
Dilution, gray, white patterns | No | Those phenotypes involve loci such as STX17, KIT, TRPM1, PAX3, MITF, RFWD3, and others.
Mane or tail color | No | No credible evidence links PRKDC to mane or tail pigment.
Height, speed, muscle, bone, hoof shape, feathering | No | No established association; these are polygenic or controlled by unrelated loci.
Immune function | Yes | Loss-of-function PRKDC causes fatal SCID in homozygotes. [cite:23]
DNA-damage repair / radiation sensitivity | Yes, biologically expected | DNA-PKcs is central to NHEJ double-strand-break repair; this is established broadly and is the mechanistic basis of the immune defect. [cite:4][cite:20]

For a coat-color simulator, model PRKDC as a health/lethality locus, not a color locus. A realistic implementation is a recessive lethal-or-near-lethal immune-failure allele with no visual phenotype in carriers.

Speculation and uncertainty
---------------------------

- Other equine PRKDC alleles: The five-base-pair deletion is the validated disease allele. There is no routinely recognized, independently validated second equine PRKDC coat-color, performance, or conformation allele. Claims that PRKDC changes color, speed, muscle, hoof shape, feathering, or mane/tail color are unsupported.
- Carrier health effects: The possible carrier association with sarcoids is speculative relative to the SCID phenotype and should not be treated as settled. [cite:16]
- Modifier effects: In humans and mice, PRKDC variation can produce a spectrum from severe immunodeficiency to milder immune, autoimmune, granulomatous, or radiosensitivity phenotypes. That does not demonstrate equivalent allelic series in horses. [cite:7][cite:10]
- Population frequency: Older prevalence and carrier-frequency figures differ widely. Modern breeding decisions should rely on current, breed-specific DNA-test results rather than historical percentages. [cite:23][cite:24]

Controversy and future science
------------------------------

The main historical controversy was not whether Arabian SCID was inherited, but which gene caused it. Early work used synteny and physical mapping to identify equine DNA-PK as a strong candidate; sequencing then established the five-base-pair deletion and loss of kinase activity as the causal defect. [cite:14]

Future work could clarify:

- Current carrier frequencies across Arabian subpopulations and Arabian-cross populations.
- Whether additional PRKDC variants exist in horses and whether any are hypomorphic rather than fully lethal.
- Whether heterozygosity has measurable effects on immune competence, cancer susceptibility, sarcoids, or response to DNA-damaging exposures.
- Whether genome editing or gene therapy could eventually prevent the immune failure, building on the extensive mammalian DNA-PKcs literature. [cite:20]

Practical testing
-----------------

UC Davis Veterinary Genetics Laboratory offers a direct SCID test and reports results as N/N, N/SCID, or SCID/SCID. Testing is most useful before breeding Arabians, part-Arabians, and horses with uncertain Arabian ancestry; avoiding carrier-to-carrier matings prevents affected foals. [cite:23][cite:11]
