Round two found no published report or laboratory page naming a fifth allele; the session applied the owner's ruling for such alleles without asking her again. The mod's estimated frequency table still gives D5 a share. Whether UC Davis has an ACAN test page was not answered. D1/D1 is "lethal_in_early_life" (owner, 2026-10-12): round two says survival time varies and gives no ages. That every other dwarf pair survives is still the mod's.
- ryr1: two papers are cited as the first description (2004 and 2009). The owner's answers disagree: the first says the 2004 paper is the primary one and 2009 may be a confusion; round two says the variant was reported in 2009 and 2004 was earlier or other work. Neither gives a source for the claim. Both stay cited.
- grey: G2/G3 is given the same outcome as G3/N; the research says its pace and risk

- silver: chromosome 6 now also rests on a university page. The one-copy eye condition (mcoa-cyst) still sits on every Z/z pair, because the format cannot tell a chestnut carrier from a bay one; round two says the risk is not established for a chestnut carrier, and the condition's text says so. That silver is hidden on a chestnut is in words only.
- leopard still depends on patn2, an abstraction; no real second modifier was found. Round two confirms that more than PATN1 modifies the pattern and names nothing.
- pdk4: G is flagged wild_type because the format needs one; no source says which allele is ancestral. Round two answered a different question: it calls PDK4 a "PSSM modifier" with an ancestral "non-risk C allele", and the variant here (A>G) has no C. Nothing was changed.
- slc5a3, ryr1, grey: the "general population" tables are still the mod's.
- Frequencies: brindle has none, on purpose. Published tables exist only for slc5a3
  (Fell, Dales), ryr1 (Quarter Horse), grey (Andalusian), b4galt7 (Friesian), dmrt3
  (Icelandic), gbe1 (Quarter Horse, Paint, Thoroughbred), myo5a (Arabian), plod1 (eight

  rapgef5 (Thoroughbred) and toe1 (Arabian).
- agouti: At (seal brown) and A+ (wild bay) are in the data marked "invalid" (owner,
  2026-10-12). The research calls them plausible but unproven rather than wrong, and
  some commercial laboratories report At; "invalid" is the nearest tag the format has.
- Bay shade is now stated nowhere as a table: agouti gives bay or black, and the
  abstracted shade locus describes its effect in its summary only. The research
  supports a real shade modifier upstream of ASIP near RALY (chromosome 22), cause
  unidentified. Round two says no dosage effect of E on bay shade is established.
- b4galt7: no chromosome was given, and round two could not verify one. Its research names its sources by number only, so
  no citation was added; the two it already had (UC Davis, Leegwater et al.) are from
  the first export. The Friesian table omits d/d horses, which the 12 percent carrier
  estimate does not count.
- ednrb and hmga2 each had an overview in round two, without addresses: chromosome, what the gene does, breeds. hmga2's overview names no tested variant and calls the gene a candidate region with a polygenic effect; the p allele rests on the earlier answer. Neither has a published frequency.
- champagne: the names gold, amber and classic champagne depend on extension and agouti; the file says so in words and has no interactions table. "Sable" (on seal brown) and "ivory" (with cream) are registry names, not alleles, and are not in the data.
- ckm: a candidate that failed to replicate (Gu et al. 2010; no association in Quarter Horses). The file states no effect. (An earlier answer's "g.22999655C>A" for it was a slip for the DMRT3 variant; settled.) C is flagged wild_type only because the format needs one. Whether a locus with no established effect belongs in the data as published is the owner's call. Round two called CKM a "tying-up risk" locus with a derived risk allele, which matches nothing in the earlier research; nothing was changed.
- dmrt3: the Icelandic table's genotype shares are worked out from one allele frequency, assuming random mating. A 2026 study reports six variants in exon 2 of DMRT3, three of them new, the new ones mainly in non-gaited breeds and of unknown effect; none is in the data. Other gait loci (trot versus pace in Standardbreds) are known to exist and are not modelled.
- dun: the wild-type flag moved from d2 to D (the research calls dun the ancestral state), and the d2/d2 outcome is now "non-dun", not "wild". nd1's markings are variable ("may show"), but d1/d1 and d1/d2 have one outcome that cannot say how often. The research gives no frequency figure, so the table is still the mod's. It gives no coordinate for the nd1 change, and round two could not verify one. Round two says d1/d1 may show markings more consistently than d1/d2, unmeasured; the two still share one outcome. It does not mention the separate TBX3 height enhancer in the earlier note; that citation (PMC8796118) is left from the first export and nothing in the data rests on it.
- gbe1: the research says G/G foals are aborted late, stillborn or die as newborns. The format has one severity per condition, so it stays "lethal_at_birth" and the pair stays a living genotype, not a nonviable one. The Quarter Horse and Paint tables give UC Davis's carrier estimates (8.3% and 7.1%, round two), which agree with twice the 2006 allele frequencies. The first answer names the 2006 study only through the Merck manual; round two calls it "Ward et al." and gives no address for it. Chromosome 26 is from round two.
- kit: homozygotes. The 30 pairs the mod's export listed as nonviable, and W4/W4, are now living-table rows with the undocumented outcome "homozygote-not-observed": the research says none has been observed and that lethality is suspected (most for W1 to W4) but unproven. There is no nonviable list at this locus any more.
- kit: 601 pairs of two different variants are "compound-undocumented". Only booster pairs are given an outcome. That an all-white variant stays all white beside a booster is reasoned, not stated.
- kit: W37, W38 and W39 are published (2025; the owner's second answer) and now have outcomes, each resting on one family or one horse; the study calls them likely causes. W37 has only been seen on the same chromosome as W35, so W37/W35 is given the W37 outcome and W37/N is in the data as if it could occur. W36 is not assigned, like W29, and there is nothing above W39. The first two answers both count 36 variants, for W1-W35 and for W1-W39; round two says the count has no stable answer.
- kit: W17 was split into W17a and W17b (owner, 2026-10-12), which the research lists as two missense variants. They are new alleles the mod lacks (mod-map.json, "not_in_mod"). The old W17 id is kept and marked "invalid"; its 41 pairs have the outcome "invalid-allele". The research does not say whether W17a and W17b are alternatives or sit together on one chromosome; they are treated as alternatives, and W17a/W17b is undocumented. Round two says they are best treated as two distinct variants and cannot say whether a horse has had both.
- kit: the research says a horse can carry several variants on one chromosome (W20 with W22, W19 with W34 and W35, W32 with W35, SB1 with W32), and the format has two alleles per locus and cannot say this. W22 is settled (round three, 2026-10-15, sourced to UC Davis and the 2024 screening study): it has never been found without W20, and UC Davis reports that chromosome as W20_22. The W22 id now stands for that haplotype, so W22/N is a real genotype (N/W20_22); round two's "W22 can occur without W20" was wrong. W20/W22 stays "boosted-white" with "usually all white" in words; the paper that found W22 calls such horses fully white, but the answer says "typically", so it was not moved to "dominant-white". The research also suggests SB1 as a locus of its own.
- kit: W4 is named Camarillo White again: round two says so, citing a laboratory's page, where the first answer had tied Camarillo White lines to W1-W4 without saying which. Round two gives W20 as chr3:7948220T>C, which looks a digit short and is not in the data. The frequency table is still the mod's; no figure was given. tobiano was given what this research says about it; it has since had research of its own.
- matp: sunshine (sun) is kept as a disputed candidate, quality "unverified", not "invalid" (owner, 2026-10-12). Its five pairs have the undocumented outcome "sunshine-disputed". The research says the variant is at allele frequencies above 0.86 in Arabians, Belgians, Morgans, Thoroughbreds and Quarter Horses; no table was made, because the figure is a lower bound and the other alleles' shares were not given. The mod's "general population" table still has sunshine as very rare, which the research contradicts. Round two adds only that sunshine is not a UC Davis allele name and that no pigment effect of its own is established.
- matp: snowdrop (sno) is a published candidate known from very few horses; UC Davis does not test for it. sno/sno and the silent carrier are given outcomes on that report alone. Cr/sno and prl/sno are "compound-undocumented".
- matp: the names palomino, buckskin, cremello and the rest depend on extension and agouti; the file says so in words and has no interactions table. The first of the three research answers marks its sources by number only, with no addresses; the citations added are the addresses in the other two. The wild type is written N here; the research also gives n and C.
- gys1: one copy can show nothing at all (incomplete penetrance), but the format has one outcome per pair, so N/PSSM1 carries the condition and its description gives the range. No frequency table was made: the figures given (87% of affected draft-related horses, 72% of affected Quarter Horse-related horses, 18% of affected Warmbloods) are shares of diagnosed cases that carry the variant, not population frequencies. Chromosome 10 is from round two. The allele id is P; UC Davis writes PSSM1.
- mushroom: the outcome depends on extension and agouti (clear on chestnut, uncertain on bay, none expected on black); the file says so in words and has no interactions table. Its link to flaxen is unresolved. No frequency was given, so the table is still the mod's "general population", though the research places the allele in Shetland Ponies and, rarely, Miniature Horses. The allele ids are Mu and mu; UC Davis writes Mu and N.
- met: met/met stays a nonviable pair. The research supports it as a recessive embryonic lethal, on indirect evidence: about 25 per cent lower insemination success in carrier-to-carrier matings. It is reported in Friesians only; the frequency table is still the mod's "general population". The research gave the UniProt record at two addresses; one is cited.
- myo5a: the locus now says it affects the coat, and the affected foal's outcome changes the coat, because the research describes the foal as diluted; the mod's export had both false. The Arabian tables use the mapping study's carrier shares (10.3% and 1.8%). The research also quotes UC Davis giving 10.3% and 1.6% as the allele's frequency; the two readings differ by a factor of two and were not reconciled; round two did not settle it, and adds a third table from another study (allele frequency 0.08, 16% carriers, sample not described). No chromosome was given, and round two could not verify one.
- plod1: affected foals may be stillborn, and the research says some pregnancies may be lost early (unproven); the single severity stays "lethal_at_birth". The breed tables are carrier shares among horses tested; the small ones (Sport Pony, Haflinger, Knabstrupper) rest on 12 to 48 horses and assume "allele detected in n" means n carriers; round two says those counts may include affected cases and are not random samples, and the tables' names say so. No chromosome was given, and round two could not verify one.
- ppib: the three Quarter Horse tables give carriers only; living affected horses exist and their share was not given, so they are counted with the clear horses. The research gives one address for all the frequency figures and does not say which study each comes from. Round two says the 28% is for affected families or high-risk lines, names no study, and gives UC Davis's PSSM1 page as its address, which is not cited. The allele id is herda; laboratories write HR or H. No chromosome was given, and round two could not verify one.
- extension: ea was added (the research: UC Davis recognises E, e and ea). It is a new allele the mod lacks (mod-map.json, "not_in_mod"). The interaction tables of agouti and flaxen gained its three pairs: ea/E as E/e, and ea/e and ea/ea as e/e. For flaxen, round two says a chestnut through ea can show flaxen. The frequency table is the mod's and has no ea; no figure was given. Chromosome 3 is from round two; no variant coordinates were given.
- extension: round two says no dosage effect of E on bay shade is established, and the summary says so; the first answer had said E/E bays may tend to be darker. It treats blood bay and liver bay as shades and seal brown (At) as unresolved, which agrees with agouti and the abstracted shade locus as they stand.
- shox: sa was split into Del1 and Del2 (owner, 2026-10-12), new alleles the mod lacks. The old sa id is kept and marked "invalid"; its four pairs have the outcome "invalid-allele", so the mod's own allele has no outcome here. The frequency table is the mod's and is still written for sa; no figure was given.
- shox has no "chromosome": the format takes a number, X or Y, and X or Y would make the locus sex-linked. It lies in the pseudoautosomal region of both and is inherited as autosomal; the summary says so.
- shox: the severity of skeletal atavism is "lethal_at_birth" (owner, 2026-10-12, changing her "impairing" of the same day): round two says affected foals are usually nonviable or put down shortly after birth. "Usually" is not "always"; the format has one severity.
- pax3: there is no nonviable list any more. SW4/SW4 is "splash-homozygote-undetected" (may be lethal, not demonstrated) and SW2/SW4 is "splash-undocumented" (never detected). No genotype carries a deafness condition: the research says SW2/SW2 horses "may be deaf" and that deafness is variable, which is the same rule mitf follows (only pairs a source confirms). The risk is in the descriptions.
- pax3: SW10 is "unconfirmed", quality "unverified", with no outcome. Round two: it is not among UC Davis's SW1-SW8, but some European laboratories list an SW9 and an SW10 with unconfirmed definitions; which gene a laboratory's SW10 lies in is not known. The two breed tables are worked out from one allele frequency each. The "general population" table is the mod's. The journal names in two citation labels were read off the addresses, not given by the research.
- tobiano: its own research and the kit research are both applied. The breeds in the summary are the two lists joined. Round two confirms about 43 Mb and gives breakpoints (chr3:42,737,476 and 79,471,993) that are about 36.7 Mb apart; both are in the summary as given, unexplained. The ink spots come from the kit research. The frequency table is the mod's; no figure was given. The allele ids are To and to; UC Davis writes TO and N.
- tiger_eye: the research says tiger eye changes the iris and never the coat. The locus still says "affects_coat": true and its eye outcomes "changes_coat": true, because the eight eye-colour loci use those flags for anything drawn on the horse; what the flags should mean for eyes is a format question for the owner. TE2/TE2 rests on one horse. The frequency table is the mod's "general population", though the variants are known only from Puerto Rican Paso Finos; no figure was given.
- st14: the severity changed from "lethal_at_birth" to "lethal_in_early_life": the research says most affected foals died between a few weeks and three years old. That is longer than the format's "first weeks or months", and one horse was alive at nearly three; it is the nearest value, chosen by the session, not the owner. The locus is still "affects_coat": false, since it removes hair and does not change colour or pattern. The Akhal-Teke table rests on 191 horses and may not be a random sample. No chromosome was given, and round two could not verify one. Most of the answer's sources are marked by number only. Round two says, without a source, that foals die within days to weeks of skin-barrier failure and infection; that contradicts the first answer and was not applied.
- prkdc: the severity changed from "lethal_at_birth" to "lethal_in_early_life": the research says affected foals generally do not survive beyond about four to six months. No frequency table was made: the research gives roughly 0.2% of Arabians affected, says carrier figures differ widely, and advises against using the old percentages; the mod's "general population" table stands. The research marks its sources by number and lists them by name only, so no citation was added; the three it already had are from the first export. The variant has no coordinates or c. name; round two could not verify any.
- rapgef5: the owner's first answer found no horse allele of RAPGEF5 at all; her second, asked about EFIH by name, confirms the variant and the disorder, and the locus follows the second. The severity changed from "lethal_at_birth" to "lethal_in_early_life": signs are reported up to about 35 days old. Two Thoroughbred tables are given because the two studies disagree (1.8% and about 0.8% allele frequency); the answer also quotes carrier figures of 1.4-1.6% without a source of their own. The answer calls the change a "C-to-A transition"; C to A is a transversion, which changes nothing here. Round two settled none of the figures: it calls them all specific to their study.
- toe1: the owner's first answer looked for a coat-colour locus and found none; her second, asked about cerebellar abiotrophy by name, confirms the disorder, and the locus follows the second. The locus is named for TOE1, but the research says the variant may act through MUTYH and that the mechanism is not settled. The Arabian table gives carriers only (about 20%); living affected horses are counted with the clear ones. The severity stays "impairing". The journal name in one citation label was read off the address.
- mstn: chromosome 18 is from round two, which calls g.66493737C>T "EquCab3-style coordinates"; which assembly the position belongs to was not checked. No frequency was given.
- scn4a: no chromosome; round two could not verify one. No frequency was given.
- lama3, lamc2: round two's table swaps the breeds (LAMA3 for Belgian drafts, LAMC2 for Saddlebreds) against the first answers and the gene files. It gives no source and may be a slip; nothing was changed.
- Breed names added to summaries in round two (dun, extension, agouti, matp, champagne, silver, roan, leopard, patn1, lcorl, mstn) are the answer's descriptions, which it says to treat as estimates. None has a figure, and no frequency table was made from them.
- Round two gave three addresses that are in no gene file: a game's wiki (horsereality) for pearl, a breeder's tool page (soloselecthorses) for fragile foal, and UC Davis's PSSM1 page for the HERDA figures.

STRUCTURE
- "chromosome" is only on loci whose research gave one. To list them:
  grep -l '"chromosome"' genes/*.json
- "sex" is not a locus here. brindle is x_linked and uses a placeholder allele "Y".
- The left-eye loci in the mod usually copy the right eye; that is not expressed here.
- tools/validate.mjs does not check that allele order is unchanged since the last
  version (there is no tagged version to compare with yet).
- 36 allele names keep the allele id, because the name alone would not tell two alleles
  of the locus apart ("Dominant white W22"). Others carry a laboratory's label in
  brackets ("Wild-type (G1)").

THE PUBLISHED REPOSITORY

  procedures/, session-logs/ and TODO.txt, which are not in the repository; each says so.

BREEDS
- breeds/ is a first export of the mod's 121 natural breeds (2026-10-14), by
  knowledge/how-this-was-exported/export-breeds.mjs. The format (knowledge/format.txt,
  BREEDS) is the session's draft; the owner has not reviewed it.
- Every pool is the mod's estimate ("estimated"), tuned for a game. Some contradict the
  published tables in the gene files: the Arabian has 2.4% toe1 carriers and 2.2% myo5a
  carriers, where genes/toe1.json gives about 20% and genes/myo5a.json 10.3% (Egyptian)
  and 1.8%. Pools were exported as the mod has them, not corrected.
- Five pools give a share to an allele this data does not confirm: andalusian (mitf SW9,
  pax3 SW10), chincoteague_pony (acan D5), gypsy_vanner (matp sno, a candidate) and
  tennessee_walking (matp sun, disputed).
- Eight pools whose weights did not sum to 100 were scaled to 100: arabian dmrt3,
  gypsy_vanner matp, karakachan extension and agouti, kiso extension and dun, morgan
  dmrt3, standardbred matp. Most were off by a rounding step; karakachan and kiso by up
  to half a percent.
- Not exported: the mod's "notes" on each breed, which hold its reasons and some real
  facts mixed with game wording; the body-size model scale; "commonness"; the heat and
  cold pools. No breed has a real height, weight, type or citation.
- "country" is the mod's id ("saudi_arabia"), not a standard code (TODO 5.8).
- welsh_pony's speed score is a range, [4, 6], as in the mod.

OPEN RESEARCH QUESTIONS
=======================
Rewritten 2026-10-12 after the owner's round-two answers (knowledge/science/round-2-answers.txt);
regrouped 2026-10-14 into blocks, one block per message to her research tool. These need
RESEARCH, not a decision from the owner. Each block stands alone: the researcher needs
nothing from this file but the PREFACE and the block. Delete a question when its answer
has been taken in, and a block when it is empty.

Why blocks: one long list of 54 questions got thin answers (nearly every frequency came
back "needs a breed survey"), and questions asked without the variant named were read as
something else (PDK4, CKM, grey). Each block below keeps one breed group or one kind of
locus together, names every variant and allele, and says where two earlier answers
disagree, so the researcher looks for the primary paper.

PREFACE (paste above every block)
---------------------------------
  I maintain an open database of horse genetics. For each question below:
  - give the address (URL or DOI) of every source you use;
  - for any frequency, say whether it is an allele frequency or a share of carriers, and
    which population, sample size and year it comes from;
  - give every genomic position with its assembly (EquCab2 or EquCab3);
  - where two earlier answers disagree, find the primary paper and say which is right;
  - if you cannot find something, say so; do not estimate.

BLOCK A - CHROMOSOMES AND VARIANTS OF INHERITED DISORDERS
---------------------------------------------------------
A1. On which horse chromosome is each of these genes, and what is the position (with
    assembly) of its tested variant?
    - B4GALT7, Friesian dwarfism, c.50G>A (p.Arg17Lys)
    - MYO5A, lavender foal syndrome, single-base deletion in exon 30 (c.4249del)
    - PLOD1, fragile foal syndrome type 1, c.2032G>A (p.Gly678Arg)
    - PPIB, HERDA, c.115G>A
    - SCN4A, hyperkalaemic periodic paralysis (HYPP)
    - ST14, naked foal syndrome, c.388G>T (p.Glu130*)
A2. What are the position and c. name of the five base-pair PRKDC deletion that causes
    SCID in Arabian horses?
A3. What is the position (with assembly) of the EDNRB p.Ile118Lys frame overo variant on
    chromosome 17?

BLOCK B - COAT COLOUR: BASE COLOURS, DILUTIONS AND GREY
-------------------------------------------------------
B1. MC1R (extension, chromosome 3): what are the positions and base changes of the red
    allele e and the rarer ea?
B2. TBX3 (dun, chromosome 8): what is the position and base change of the nd1 variant?
    (nd2 is a 1,617 bp deletion.)
B3. What allele frequencies are published, by breed, with the study for each, for:
    - dun: D, nd1 and nd2;
    - extension: e and ea;
    - agouti: the recessive black allele a (ASIP c.191_201del);
    - SLC45A2: cream (c.457G>A), pearl (c.985G>A) and sunshine (c.568G>A);
    - champagne (SLC36A1 c.188C>G);
    - silver (PMEL Arg618Cys);
    - mushroom (MFSD12 c.600dupC) in Shetland Ponies and Miniature Horses;
    - tiger eye (SLC24A5, TE1 and TE2) in the Puerto Rican Paso Fino.
B4. Snowdrop (SLC45A2 c.305G>A): has it been confirmed in more horses than the first
    report? What does a horse with one cream allele and snowdrop look like, and one with
    pearl and snowdrop?
B5. Grey (STX17): the G2 allele carries two copies of the 4.6 kb duplication and G3
    carries three. How fast does a horse with one G2 and one G3 grey, and what is its
    melanoma risk, compared with G2/N and G3/N?

BLOCK C - WHITE PATTERNS: KIT, TOBIANO AND SPLASH WHITE
-------------------------------------------------------
C1. KIT W20: one answer gives its position as chr3:7948220T>C, which looks a digit short
    beside W22 at chr3:79548925. What is the correct position, and on which assembly?
C3. KIT W37: has it been found without W35 on the same chromosome? KIT W17a and W17b: has
    any horse been found with both?
C4. Tobiano: the inversion near KIT is described as about 43 Mb, but its breakpoints are
    given as chr3:42,737,476 and chr3:79,471,993, about 36.7 Mb apart. Which is right,
    and on which assembly?
C5. Splash white: some European laboratories (for example Genomia) list "SW9" and "SW10"
    beside UC Davis's SW1 to SW8. What variant is each - gene, change and the paper that
    describes it?
C6. What allele frequencies are published, by breed, for KIT SB1, W5, W10, W13, W20, W22,
    W32, W34 and W35, and for the MITF splash alleles SW1, SW3, SW5, SW6, SW7 and SW8?

BLOCK D - BODY SIZE, PERFORMANCE AND GAIT
-----------------------------------------
D1. HMGA2: what is the variant that reduces height in Shetland ponies and other small
    horses (Frischknecht et al. 2015) - its name and position?
D2. MSTN g.66493737C>T (the "speed gene" of Thoroughbreds): is that position on EquCab2 or
    EquCab3?
D3. PDK4 g.38973231A>G and CKM g.15884567G>A are candidate racing-performance variants,
    not disease variants. For each, which allele is ancestral?
D4. What allele frequencies are published, by breed, for LCORL (marker BIEC2-808543),
    HMGA2, MSTN g.66493737C>T, PDK4 g.38973231A>G and CKM g.15884567G>A?
D5. DMRT3: a 2026 study reported six variants in exon 2, three of them new. What are
    they, and is any effect shown? Which named loci, besides DMRT3 c.902C>A, decide
    whether a Standardbred trots or paces?

BLOCK E - DISORDERS OF ARABIANS AND THOROUGHBREDS
-------------------------------------------------
E1. Lavender foal syndrome (MYO5A): in the study that mapped the variant (Brooks et al.
    2010), are the figures 10.3% (Egyptian Arabians) and 1.8% or 1.6% (other Arabians)
    allele frequencies or shares of carriers? A second study gives an allele frequency
    of 0.08 and 16% carriers: what horses did it sample?
E2. Cerebellar abiotrophy (TOE1 g.2171G>A): about 20% of Arabians are carriers. What
    share is affected (two copies)?
E3. SCID (PRKDC): what is the carrier frequency in Arabians today, from a named study?
E4. EFIH (RAPGEF5 c.2624C>A): which study gives a Thoroughbred allele frequency of 1.8%
    and which about 0.8%, and what is the source of the figure "1.4-1.6% carriers"?

BLOCK F - DISORDERS OF QUARTER HORSES AND RELATED BREEDS
--------------------------------------------------------
F1. GBED (GBE1 c.102C>A): which 2006 study gave allele frequencies of 4.1% in Quarter
    Horses and 3.6% in Paint Horses? Its authors and address, please.
F2. HERDA (PPIB c.115G>A): which studies give carrier frequencies of 3.5% (United
    States), 1.6% (France) and about 28% (cutting lines)? What share of horses is
    affected (two copies)?
F3. What carrier frequencies are published, by breed, for PSSM1 (GYS1 c.925G>A), HYPP
    (SCN4A) and frame overo (EDNRB p.Ile118Lys)?
F4. Malignant hyperthermia (RYR1 c.7360C>G): which paper first reported it in horses -
    Aleman et al. 2004 (Muscle and Nerve) or the 2009 paper in the Journal of Veterinary
    Internal Medicine? Two earlier answers disagree.

BLOCK G - DISORDERS OF WARMBLOODS, DRAFTS, PONIES AND OTHER BREEDS
------------------------------------------------------------------
G1. Junctional epidermolysis bullosa: which gene carries the form found in Belgian and
    related draft horses, and which the form in the American Saddlebred? Earlier answers
    say LAMC2 for the drafts and LAMA3 for the Saddlebred; a later one has them the other
    way round. What carrier frequencies are published for each?
G2. Fragile foal syndrome (PLOD1 c.2032G>A): what are the carrier frequencies for each
    Warmblood breed, from which studies? Are the Knabstrupper (3 of 46), Haflinger (2 of
    48) and American Sport Pony (1 of 12) figures carriers or affected cases, and from
    which study?
G3. Naked foal syndrome (ST14 c.388G>T, Akhal-Teke): at what age do affected foals die,
    and of what? The paper that found the variant is reported as saying a few weeks to
    three years, cause unresolved; a later answer said days to weeks, of skin-barrier
    failure and infection. Is there a breed-wide carrier figure beyond the study's 26 of
    191?
G4. Friesian dwarfism (B4GALT7): how was the figure of 12% carriers sampled, and what is
    it now? The MET embryonic lethal in Friesians: what carrier frequency is published?
G5. Skeletal atavism (SHOX deletions Del1 and Del2) in Shetland Ponies and Miniature
    Horses: what carrier frequencies are published?
G6. ACAN dwarfism (variants D1 to D4, Miniature Horses and ponies): how long do D1/D1
    foals live? Do horses with two other variants (D2, D3, D4 in any combination, or with
    D1) live to adulthood? What carrier frequencies are published? Does UC Davis have a
    test page for ACAN dwarfism, and which variants does it name?
