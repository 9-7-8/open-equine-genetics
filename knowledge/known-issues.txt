STILL WRONG OR UNPROVEN IN THE RESEARCHED LOCI
- abstracted_jump_strength was "ryr2" until 2026-10-12 (owner: rename it). The data has two abstractions for jumping, this and abstracted_jump; whether both are wanted is open.
- acan: D5 is "unconfirmed" with no outcome: no published report or laboratory page names a fifth allele, and Generatio's test lists only D1-D4. The estimated "general population" table still gives D5 a share. UC Davis has no ACAN test page. Two of Generatio's four positions do not match the file's D3 and D4 (question G7); the round-three answers write D3 as "D3*". D1/D1, D1/D2 and D1/D3 are nonviable, dying before birth (round three, Scientific Reports 2020); D1/D4 is a living pair with its own outcome, usually lost before birth but known once from a live foal (the format cannot give a pair a chance of surviving). Pairs of D2, D3 and D4 are born alive and can reach adulthood. The four alleles together are at about 0.28 in Miniature Horses, not split by allele, so no table (question G6).
- agouti: At (seal brown) and A+ (wild bay) are marked "invalid" (owner, 2026-10-12). The research calls them plausible but unproven rather than wrong, and some laboratories report At; "invalid" is the nearest tag the format has.
- b4galt7: its first research names its sources by number only. The Friesian tables omit d/d horses. The 12% is Leegwater et al. 2016's 22 of 177, a validation set, not a random sample; KFPS testing gave about 15% in 2000 and 12.5% in 2021 (Hermans et al. 2024). A Brazilian 33% (12 horses) is not a table.
- champagne: the names gold, amber and classic champagne depend on extension and agouti; the file says so in words and has no interactions table. "Sable" and "ivory" are registry names, not alleles.
- ckm: a candidate that failed to replicate (Gu et al. 2010; no association in Quarter Horses); the file states no effect. Its reference allele is flagged wild_type and called ancestral on that alone. Round three puts CKM on chromosome 9, the file says 10; and an Italian Trotter study's marker g.16079732G>A may or may not be the file's g.15884567G>A (question D6). Whether a locus with no established effect belongs in the data as published is the owner's call.
- dmrt3: the Icelandic table is worked out from one allele frequency. Of six exon-2 variants in a 2026 study, only c.902C>A has an established effect; three are not named in the summary the research could reach. Trot versus pace in Standardbreds is predicted by a seven-SNP panel of putative modifiers (McCoy et al. 2019); not modelled.
- dun: nd1's markings are variable ("may show"), but d1/d1 and d1/d2 share one outcome that cannot say how often; round two says d1/d1 may mark more consistently, unmeasured. The nd1 change is G>T at chr8:18,227,267+1,066 on EquCab2.0, inside the 1.6 kb sequence the reference lacks, so it has no plain reference position. Breed tables exist for the Norwegian Fjord and Gypsy Vanner only (question B3). A citation to a TBX3 height enhancer (PMC8796118) has nothing in the data resting on it.
- ednrb: the EDNRB gene span an answer gave is on another assembly (NC_091700.1) and is not in the data.
- extension: the "general population" table has no ea. Breed tables exist for the Pony of the Americas only; ea is at 0.035 in Knabstruppers, with no e figure beside it (question B3).
- gbe1: G/G foals are aborted late, stillborn or die as newborns; the format has one severity per condition, so it stays "lethal_at_birth" and the pair stays living, not nonviable. The Quarter Horse and Paint tables give UC Davis's carrier estimates (8.3%, 7.1%); UC Davis now gives 8-11% by discipline.
- grey: G2/G3 has the G3/N outcome, as UC Davis states. The Andalusian table, and ryr1's Quarter Horse table, are worked out from allele frequencies.
- gys1: one copy can show nothing (incomplete penetrance), but the format has one outcome per pair, so N/P carries the condition and its text gives the range. Drafts are given only as a 36-92% range, not tabled. The allele id is P; UC Davis writes PSSM1.
- hmga2: the estimated "general population" table gives p a 25% allele share, though the allele is found in no full-sized breed tested.
- kit: homozygotes of 30 variants, and W4/W4, are living rows with the undocumented outcome "homozygote-not-observed": none has been observed, and lethality is suspected (most for W1-W4) but unproven.
- kit: 601 pairs of two different variants are "compound-undocumented". Only booster pairs have an outcome; that an all-white variant stays all white beside a booster is reasoned, not stated.
- kit: horses can carry several variants on one chromosome (W20 with W22, W35 with W37, W19 with W34 and W35, W32 with W35, SB1 with W32); the format has two alleles per locus and cannot say this. W22 stands for the W20_22 haplotype (it is never found without W20). W37 has only been seen with W35, yet W37/N is in the data as if it could occur. W20/W22 is "boosted-white", "usually all white" in words. The research suggests SB1 as a locus of its own.
- kit: W17 is the allele again, two changes always together (owner, 2026-10-15); W17a and W17b are invalid. The OMIA position quoted for it does not match the c. changes base for base and is not in the data.
- kit: W37, W38 and W39 (2025) each rest on one family or one horse. W29 and W36 are not assigned; nothing above W39. W20's EquCab3.0 position is printed a digit short in the 2024 paper; chr3:79482200T>C is a reconstruction (question C1). No KIT position is in the data. No breed table: no breed has every KIT allele's share (question C6).
- lama3, lamc2: no frequency question left; JEB1 and JEB2 tables are in.
- leopard depends on patn2, an abstraction; more than PATN1 modifies the pattern, and nothing else is named.
- matp: sunshine is a disputed candidate with the undocumented outcome "sunshine-disputed". One answer puts it at allele frequencies above 0.86 in several breeds; another calls it very rare in one lineage (question B3). Snowdrop is known from one Gypsy Cob family; Cr/sno and prl/sno are undocumented (question B4). The names palomino, buckskin and the rest depend on extension and agouti, in words only. No breed table: no breed has both Cr and prl given (question B3). The first matp answer cites by number only.
- met: met/met is nonviable on indirect evidence (lower insemination success in carrier matings). Reported in Friesians only; no frequency (question G4). Friesian hydrocephalus (B3GALNT2) is not a locus here.
- mitf: SW9 (McFadden et al. 2023) rests on association in one breed. 24 pairs of two different variants are "splash-undocumented" and 6 homozygotes "splash-homozygote-undetected". No genotype carries a deafness condition (owner: only pairs a source confirms). No breed table (question C6).
- mushroom: the outcome depends on extension and agouti (clear on chestnut, uncertain on bay, none on black), in words only. Its link to flaxen is unresolved.
- myo5a: UC Davis's "1.6%" for non-Egyptian Arabians matches neither of the mapping study's figures and is left out. The Egyptian farm table (0.08) is one farm, partly targeted.
- pax3: SW10 rests on association in one breed. SW4/SW4 and SW10/SW10 are "splash-homozygote-undetected"; pairs of two variants "splash-undocumented". No deafness condition on any pair; the risk is in the text. The journal names in two citation labels were read off the addresses.
- pdk4: A is flagged wild_type (reference allele, carried by pre-domestication horses); no formal reconstruction was found.
- plod1: affected foals may be stillborn and some pregnancies may be lost early (unproven); the severity stays "lethal_at_birth". No EquCab3.0 position for c.2032G>A. Several Warmblood breeds have no figure (question G2).
- ppib: the Quarter Horse tables give carriers only; living affected horses are counted with the clear ones, and no observed affected share is published. One table comes from a research-summary site.
- prkdc: no frequency table: about 0.2% of Arabians affected is all that was given (question E3). The research names its sources by number only.
- rapgef5: three Thoroughbred tables from three studies, all kept.
- roan: the symbol stays KIT; the causal variant is unknown. RN1-RN3 are haplotype markers, in the summary only.
- ryr1, slc5a3, grey: their "general population" tables are estimates.
- shox: no "chromosome": it lies in the pseudoautosomal region and is inherited as autosomal; the format's X or Y would make it sex-linked. The severity "lethal_at_birth" stands for "usually nonviable or put down"; the format has one severity. The "general population" table is still written for the invalid sa.
- silver: the one-copy eye condition sits on every Z/z pair, because the format cannot tell a chestnut carrier from a bay one; the risk is not established for a chestnut carrier, and the text says so.
- st14: "lethal_in_early_life" was the session's choice, the nearest value for death between a few weeks and three years. The Akhal-Teke table (26 of 191) is the only survey. Most of the first answer's sources are numbered only.
- tiger_eye: the research says tiger eye changes the iris, never the coat, but the locus says "affects_coat": true, as all eight eye loci do for anything drawn on the horse; what the flag means for eyes is a format question for the owner. TE2/TE2 rests on one horse. No population frequency (question B3).
- tobiano: whether the inversion begins upstream or downstream of KIT, and whether TO/TO is whiter than TO/N, is unsettled (question C4).
- toe1: the variant may act through MUTYH; the mechanism is not settled. The Egyptian sample (6.25% carriers) is far below the Arabian table's 20%.
- Breed names given in round-two summaries are the answer's descriptions, to be treated as estimates.
- Round two gave three addresses in no gene file: a game's wiki (horsereality) for pearl, a breeder's tool page for fragile foal, and UC Davis's PSSM1 page for HERDA.

OPEN RESEARCH QUESTIONS
=======================
Rewritten 2026-10-15 after round three. These need RESEARCH, not a decision from the
owner. Send one block per message to her research tool, with the PREFACE above it; each
block stands alone. Delete a question when its answer has been taken in, and a block when

What round three taught: name the gene AND the variant in every question (G4, asked as
"MET", came back about a different disorder); ask for every allele of a locus at once
when a table is wanted (single-allele figures cannot be tabled); and ask for an observed

PREFACE (paste above every block)
---------------------------------
  I maintain an open database of horse genetics. For each question below:
  - give the address (URL or DOI) of every source you use;
  - for any frequency, say whether it is an allele frequency or a share of carriers, and
    which population, sample size and year it comes from;
  - give every genomic position with its assembly (EquCab2 or EquCab3);
  - where two earlier answers disagree, find the primary paper and say which is right;
  - if you cannot find something, say so; do not estimate, predict or model.

BLOCK B - COAT COLOUR: BASE COLOURS AND DILUTIONS
-------------------------------------------------
B3. Avila et al. 2022 (Genes 13:1641, PMC9498372) gives allele frequencies by breed, some
    in its supplement. For each breed, give the frequencies of ALL alleles of a locus
    together, so a full table can be made:
    - dun (TBX3): D, nd1 and nd2 (the PRE figures given so far, nd1 0.79 and nd2 0.20,
      leave 0.01 unexplained);
    - extension (MC1R): e and ea together, especially for Knabstrupper, Paint Horse,
      Percheron and Quarter Horse;
    - SLC45A2: cream (Cr) and pearl (prl) together, for each breed with either.
    Also:
    - sunshine (SLC45A2 c.568G>A): one earlier answer put it at allele frequencies above
      0.86 in several breeds (PMC11006912); another calls it very rare, in one lineage.
      Which is right, from the primary papers?
    - tiger eye (SLC24A5, TE1 and TE2): a frequency in the Puerto Rican Paso Fino as a
      whole, not only among tiger-eyed horses.
B4. Snowdrop (SLC45A2 c.305G>A, also written c.449G>A; known from one Gypsy Cob family):
    has any horse with cream and snowdrop (Cr/sno), or pearl and snowdrop (prl/sno), been
    tested and described - in a paper, a laboratory case or a registry? Name the horse or
    the report. An expected phenotype is not wanted.

BLOCK C - WHITE PATTERNS: KIT, TOBIANO AND SPLASH WHITE
-------------------------------------------------------
C1. KIT W20 (c.2045G>A, p.Arg682His): the 2024 KIT haplotype paper (PMC10854588) prints
    chr3:7948220T>C, a digit short. Does any source (Ensembl, dbSNP/EVA, UC Davis or
    another paper) print its EquCab3.0 position in full? It is thought to be
    chr3:79482200T>C, but that was reconstructed, not read anywhere.
C4. Tobiano (inversion chr3:42,737,476_79,471,993 on EquCab3.0): earlier answers place it
    upstream of KIT; the latest says it begins about 100 kb downstream. Which is right,
    by the primary paper (Brooks et al., PMID 18253033, or the 2024 review, PMC10854722)?
    And do TO/TO horses have measurably more white than TO/N, or do they not reliably
    differ? Earlier answers say the second; the latest says "often more white".
C6. For each breed in Avila et al. 2022 (PMC9498372, including its supplement): the shares
    of ALL its KIT variants together (SB1, W5, W10, W13, W20, W22, W32, W34, W35 and any
    other), and of all its MITF splash variants (SW1, SW3, SW5-SW9). Does the study's W20
    figure include the W20 carried on W22 (W20_22) chromosomes? And what is the frequency
    of SW10 (PAX3 c.927C>T) in Pura Raza Espanola horses (McFadden et al. 2023)?
